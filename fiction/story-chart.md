# Milo Hale — story chart

A LitRPG / portal-fantasy / M-M romance drafted inside the Scattered Stars setting. Second person, past tense, British spellings. A man from Warrington, 2025, arrives in Brovdingonai in 3862 C.

Not lore. Nothing in this directory is canon; it draws on canon and should be corrected against it, never the other way round.

**Drafted:** chapters one to seven, Thall 1/3 to Thall 2/3. Act one closed with Carnival; the middle is running.

---

## Governing rules

- **The System's honesty rule is now broken, and that's the point.** For the first three chapters the interface never had information Milo lacked — it guessed, admitted it was guessing, and used his old employer's ticket template because it was made out of the inside of his head. From chapter four it does things no information could do. Every capability costs him the same thing: the arrival nosebleed. Ability and transit must stay the same phenomenon — the griddle catch went off "like the road," and that equivalence is the spine of the mystery.
- **The cover works because it is structurally the same shape as the truth.** A memory-haven Drifter and a man from 2025 have identical answers to almost every question. Milo almost never lies about content, only about frame. That is what makes it survivable and what makes it damning.
- **Arrhene betrayal is secrecy, not sex.** Canon is explicit: "The injuries that end Arrhene households are the quiet ones." The romance's obstacle is not a rival or a taboo — the one thing this culture cannot forgive is the one thing Milo is entirely made of. He turned down four seconds on a storeroom step for exactly that reason and it will keep being the right decision for as long as he keeps not telling.
- **Nobody hunts him. Systems process him.** The threat is never a pursuer; it is a queue, a backlog, an exception nobody has got to yet. A man who spent four years clearing other people's anomalies at three in the morning knows precisely what happens next, which is: nothing, for six weeks.
- **No Planet of Hats.** Every institution is under-resourced, argued about, and staffed by people with mixed motives. The inspector doesn't catch him because he's tired and on his eleventh pitch.
- **The interface answers clean intention, never vague wanting.** Established on the medical table; it is his whole character arc in one mechanic.

## Medium term — the middle of the book

- **The three files.** 3798, 3831, 3844: same structure, same interval, same rounding habit, three unconnected clients, sixty years apart — which is longer than one man's working life unless he starts at twenty and stops at eighty, and men here do. Sey's public answer was *a training lineage, it is a Tuesday*; his private position is nineteen years of knowing. The Banking War arbitration dockets grind on until 3890. A young analyst finding the wrong ledger is a whole novel, and Sey put that screening page on a labour board for a hundred and eighteen days.
- **The grey market.** Sorian is the door. Torcs with the tracking stripped, records that can be reached — Thales Copper-Hand does the same trade in Landwick and the Shambles backroom men keep evening hours. Every use of it puts Milo further outside the registry route he actually needs.
- **The registry.** Three ways to become a person: the Ministry (real scrutiny), the grey market (now open, and corrosive), or a household. Marriage is legally the formation of a household — so the romance and the identity problem eventually become the same problem at the worst possible moment.
- **Corin's notebook.** Sixteen, quiet, writes everything down, and now holds two things: a bearing Milo could not give, and seventy kilos that did not land. He is the one who works it out, not the adults, and the crisis is whether he tells.
- **Real Drift.** A haven ship, an enclave, a Drifter at Verenstad Gamma. The cover dies the first time it meets someone who actually is one — unless that person covers for him, which is the Drift's whole creed.
- **The phone.** Sorian has a buyer and declined to determine whether it is genuine. He will have to choose again, and Milo is now in his debt twice.

## Long term

- **Why him.** The Empty Quarter Event (3644 C — eleven systems, forty million people, infrastructure intact and unattended) rhymes with the shape of his arrival: a subtraction with no edges. Keep it unsolved, per canon; let the rhyme do the work. The Lazarus Anomalies stay untouched.
- **Can he go back, and does he want to.** The answer should get worse as his life here gets better.
- **The System's owner.** `escalation path: —` is the joke and the horror.
- **The arc.** He asked for printed rules. He gets them, and they nearly ruin him, because Arrhenos runs on manners rather than locks — a door with no lock, a double knock, and a wait. The ending is him acting without consulting the interface.

## Continuity ledger

**Arrival.** Wednesday 15 October 2025, 05:11, a flooded underpass outside Warrington. Lands on the Fountain Steps, Level 2 of the Shambles, Brovdingonai, on Thall 1/3, 3862 C. Chapters run Thall 1/3, 1/3, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 1/6–2/2, 2/2–2/3.

**The cover.** Drift, off a memory haven called *Ninth Hour* — the name of his old guild, said under pressure to Tobin Cray. Corin has checked the public register: no such haven, which proves nothing, and Corin said so himself. Haven has moved on; filed *unreachable*.

**The torc.** Good hardware from Iolas Mern, registered against a case number and not a person. Lex upgraded two things on the specification while Milo's eyes were shut; Milo has still not found out. Bram's own layer is far better than a hired kitchen and a six-stool counter should afford, and nobody has remarked on it.

**The phone.** With Sorian. 9,400 advanced, 300 for the extraction, five per cent compounding per thirty days, 180-day window, roughly 12,000 to clear. Sealed valuation was 14,000; Milo took 4,600 less to keep 3,208 photographs and 41 videos, now in the torc.

**Money.** 1,584 after chapter five; +1,100 taken from Bram's float (of 4,200 pressed on him three times); ends chapter seven at 2,684. Wage 12/hour, six days, plus a paid week off. *Known wobble:* the closing panel of chapter three says 2,760 and chapter four opens at 1,890 — the three weeks at the racks needs squaring on a revision pass.

**The clocks.** 173 days to redeem the phone. 354 days to Sey's attestation. Six to eight weeks until somebody opens the exception. The wage services none of them.

**The System.** Named Thall 1/5. Inventory: six slots, ceiling around what fits in two hands (broken once, at seventy kilos, at the cost of a near-blackout), no inference on recall, works on living things, nothing inside changes temperature. Retention: exact columns held indefinitely. Cost is the arrival nosebleed, scaling with use, and it compounds — six days of Carnival put bleeds in both nostrils and one ear.

*Current slots:* 1 float (returned), 2 knife, 3 unlicensed jar, 4 **live griddle unit, 70 kg, hot**, 5–6 empty.

**Language.** Plateau tongue on the street, Campottonì Standard in the records. Words understood: 1 → 11 → 47 → 93 → 414. Spoken: 2 → about eighty, which is exactly what a counter needs. The half-second flick is gone at a counter, about three things, and everywhere else it remains.

## Craft notes

- Three registers, kept typographically distinct: prose (second person, past), System panels (fenced blocks, dotted leaders, lowercase commentary under uppercase fields), and machine translation (inline backticks, `[ ]` gaps, archaic register because the corpus is archival).
- Scene breaks are `⁂`. From chapter six, bolded day markers carry the pace; chapters seven onward run shorter and cut harder.
- Things that survived the gap, used as structural rhyme: the green cross, the metre and the kilogram, the word *quid*.
- The recurring number is eleven. It started as a lighting cycle in an empty office outside Warrington.
