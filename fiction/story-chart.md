# Milo Hale — story chart

A LitRPG / portal-fantasy / M-M romance drafted inside the Scattered Stars setting. Second person, past tense, British spellings. A man from Warrington, 2025, arrives in Brovdingonai in 3862 C.

Not lore. Nothing in this directory is canon; it draws on canon and should be corrected against it, never the other way round.

---

## Governing rules

- **The System's honesty rule is now half-broken, and that's the point.** For the first three chapters the interface never had information Milo lacked — it guessed, admitted it was guessing, and used his old employer's ticket template because it was made out of the inside of his head. From chapter four it does things no information could do. Every capability costs him the same thing: the arrival nosebleed. Ability and transit must stay the same phenomenon.
- **The cover works because it is structurally the same shape as the truth.** A memory-haven Drifter and a man from 2025 have identical answers to almost every question — no papers, an archaic language, baseline enhancement, an unreachable home. Milo almost never lies about content, only about frame. That is what makes it survivable and what makes it damning.
- **Arrhene betrayal is secrecy, not sex.** Canon is explicit: "The injuries that end Arrhene households are the quiet ones." So the romance's obstacle is not a rival or a taboo — it is that the one thing this culture cannot forgive is the one thing Milo is entirely made of. The Ossen dissolution in chapter five states the lesson out loud and he does nothing about it.
- **No Planet of Hats.** Every institution he touches is under-resourced, argued about, and staffed by people with mixed motives. The clinic is a walk-in. The labour hall is a labour hall. The best man in the office is the one who says nothing for four hours.
- **The interface responds to clean intention, never to vague wanting.** Established on the medical table in chapter two, and it is the whole of his character arc in one mechanic: he spent thirty-one years half-wanting things, and the System will not answer half-wanting.

## Medium term — the middle of the book

- **The wrong ledger.** Reconciliation work is a door into the Banking War arbitration dockets, which grind on until 3890. Milo can hold whole files at once and compare things nobody would ever put side by side. Three files with the same hand are already in a box on the long wall: 3798, 3831, 3844.
- **Corin's notebook.** Sixteen, quiet, writes everything down, has one inconsistency already — Milo could not say which way his haven went. He is the one who works it out, not the adults, and the crisis is whether he tells.
- **Elio from the data side.** A man whose job is making incompatible records agree, who likes Milo, and who will one day pull a thread out of a medical file.
- **The registry.** Three routes: the Ministry (real scrutiny), the grey market (Reyk warned him off; Sorian is adjacent), or a household. Marriage is legally the formation of a household — so the romance and the identity problem eventually become the same problem, at the worst possible moment.
- **Real Drift.** A haven ship, an enclave, a Drifter at Verenstad Gamma. The cover dies the first time it meets someone who actually is one — unless that person chooses to cover for him, which is the Drift's whole creed.
- **The phone.** Sorian has a buyer. Sorian has also declined to determine whether it is genuine. He will have to choose again.

## Long term

- **Why him.** The Empty Quarter Event (3644 C — eleven systems, forty million people, infrastructure intact and unattended) rhymes with the shape of his arrival: a subtraction with no edges. Keep it unsolved, per canon. Let the rhyme do the work. The Lazarus Anomalies stay untouched.
- **Can he go back, and does he want to.** The real question, and the answer should get worse as his life here gets better.
- **The System's owner.** `escalation path: —` is the joke and the horror. A man who spent his life answering for systems he did not build, holding one nobody will answer for.
- **The arc.** He asked for printed rules. He gets them, and they nearly ruin him, because Arrhenos runs on manners rather than locks — a door with no lock, a double knock, and a wait. The ending is him acting without consulting the interface.

## Continuity ledger

**Arrival.** Wednesday 15 October 2025, 05:11, a flooded underpass outside Warrington. Lands on the Fountain Steps, Level 2 of the Shambles, Brovdingonai, on Thall 1/3, 3862 C. Chapters two, three, four and five run Thall 1/3, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5. Carnival begins Thall 1/7.

**The cover.** Drift, off a memory haven called *Ninth Hour* — the name of his old guild, said under pressure to Tobin Cray and now permanent. Haven has moved on; filed *unreachable*. Robbed. Kell built all of it; Milo only ever declined to contradict him.

**The torc.** Loaner from the clinic, replaced by good hardware from Iolas Mern, registered against a case number and not a person. Lex upgraded two things on the specification while Milo's eyes were shut; Milo does not find out for a fortnight.

**The phone.** With Sorian. 9,400 advanced, 300 charged for the extraction, five per cent compounding on each thirty days, one hundred and eighty days to redeem, roughly twelve thousand to clear it. Sealed valuation was 14,000; he took 4,600 less to keep 3,208 photographs and 41 videos, now in the torc. Paper ticket, in the inventory. Sorian will not determine whether it is genuine and says so.

**Money.** 9,400 − 300 − 6,260 (repaying Lex) − lodging. End of chapter four: 1,612. End of chapter five: 1,584. Wage twelve the hour, six days. *Known wobble:* the closing panel of chapter three says 2,760 and chapter four opens at 1,890; the three weeks at the racks needs to be squared on a revision pass.

**The clocks.** 178 days to redeem the phone (≈12,000 quid). 359 days to Sey's attestation. The wage services neither.

**The System.** Named at half past five on the morning of Thall 1/5. Inventory: six items, ceiling around what fits in two hands, no inference on recall, works on living things, nothing inside changes temperature, costs a nosebleed and a bad head. Retention: exact columns held indefinitely, same cost. Every use is the arrival nosebleed at a smaller scale — left nostril, flashbulb behind the eyes.

**Language.** Plateau tongue on the street, Campottonì Standard in the records; he has been learning the wrong one. Words understood: 1 → 11 → 47 → 93. Words spoken: 2. The public translation layer drops one word in six, renders untranslatables as `[ ]`, lags half a second on push and two to four on the desk puck, and calls a torc a *neck-ring*. Bram's layer is much better than a hired kitchen and a six-stool counter should be able to afford, and nobody has remarked on it.

**Objects still in play.** Lanyard, keys, wallet (four cards, £11.50), the pawn ticket — all in the inventory. Half a petrol-station sandwich, eaten. The contact Lex pushed to his torc. Three files on the long wall at Sey & Halloway: 3798, 3831, 3844, same hand, put back in the right place.

## Craft notes

- Three registers, kept typographically distinct: prose (second person, past), System panels (fenced blocks, dotted leaders, lowercase commentary under uppercase fields), and machine translation (inline backticks, `[ ]` gaps, occasional archaic register because the corpus is archival).
- Scene breaks are `⁂`.
- Things that survived the gap, used as structural rhyme: the green cross, the metre and the kilogram, the word *quid*.
- The half-second flick down and left — everyone reading his subtitles before they answer — is a permanent tax until he actually learns the language. He never gets to see what his own words turned into on the way.
