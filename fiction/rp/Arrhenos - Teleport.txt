I have a go but am quickly relegated to being the most experienced guys' assistant. They get it active and direct me to stand in a light-marked square on the floor. With a flash I'm teleported a few feet across the room as planned. However, a fault causes me to reappear shrunk, only a few inches tall!

==

They determine that an inefficiency caused most of my mass to be wasted. They can't reverse it without a massive energy source as well as some equipment they can only get portside when we next dock in a couple of weeks. They carry me to mess to have this discussion over food.

==

I discover over the next days how the crewmen instinctively to deal with me day-to-day. It involves a lot of manhandling and rather humiliating or intimate interactions, especially in the showers and in the barracks, where one of the guys often decides for fun to lodge me somewhere on his person overnight, whether between his pecs, in his armpit, or under his waistband.
I find it increasingly difficult not to immediately obey any direct command from a full-size man, which at some point I deduce has something to do with pheromones. The insidious part is how natural the process of subordinating my will and sense of self to theirs feels from the inside.

==

A cargo hauler named Ajax, one of the tallest and beefiest men on the thousands-strong crew, takes to my objectification like a pro, and others follow his lead. With disorienting speed he monopolises me, the others finding it much more convenient to not have to think about me and let Ajax have me. He starts floating the idea that I might want to stay tiny, in unnoticeable and plausibly deniable contexts, to get people used to the notion without bothering to ask me about it.

==

By the time of the shore leave, everyone has convinced themselves that they've heard me ask to stay small indefinitely, and the idea of working towards regrowing me doesn't even come up. At some point a crewman files an official reclassification of me from crewman to tool used for small-space engineering work. The stopover in the Arrhene-owned space-station is a whirlwind of letting off steam and revelry.
