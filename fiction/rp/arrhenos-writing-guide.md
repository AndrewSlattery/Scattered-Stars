Writing Guide: Arrhenos
Target Audience: Writers contributing to the Scattered Stars setting.
Subject: Arrhenos (System: Mas)
Arrhenos is unique in the Scattered Stars setting: a wealthy, stable, meritocratic society comprised entirely of men. This document outlines the specific tonal, linguistic, and social rules required to portray this world authentically.
1. The Gender Paradigm
The most immediate characteristic of Arrhenos is the absence of women. This is not an accident or a curse; it is a foundational, deliberate social structure established 900+ years ago.
The "He/Him" Default
 * Universal Pronouns: In Arrhene languages, there is only one set of animate pronouns. When writing in English, use he/him for every Arrhene character.
 * No Female Coding: Avoid gendered terms that imply a female counterpart unless specifically referencing off-worlders.
   * Avoid: "Sister," "Mother," "Aunt," "Niece," "Wife," "Queen," "Waitress," "Actress."
   * Use: "Brother," "Father," "Uncle," "Nephew," "Husband/Spouse," "Symposiarch," "Server," "Performer."
 * Off-Worlders: Women exist elsewhere (the Mandate, Vega, etc.). Arrhene men know what women are; they just don't live with them.
   * Reaction: An Arrhene man seeing a woman (e.g., on Gamma station) might react with mild curiosity or indifference, but rarely shock. They are viewed as "foreign" rather than "alien."
   * Gamma Exception: The orbital station Gamma is cosmopolitan. Women (tourists, diplomats) are seen there. On the planetary surface, they are virtually non-existent.
The "Boy" Dynamic
 * Childhood: Society is full of boys. Schools, playgrounds, and streets are populated by male children.
 * Reference: A character might refer to "when I was a boy," but never "when I was a kid" if it obscures the gendered reality. The specificity of boyhood is central to the culture.
2. Family and Reproduction
Arrhenos has decoupled reproduction from sexuality and parenting from romance.
The Continuity Programme
 * Origin: All children are born via artificial gestation (the Continuity Programme).
 * Access: Any adult citizen can apply for a son. It is a bureaucratic/medical process, not a biological accident.
 * Biology vs. Role: "Father" means "the man raising the child." Genetic contribution is often anonymized or mixed. Do not have characters obsess over "bloodlines" unless they are eccentric traditionalists.
Family Structures (Crucial!)
DO NOT default to the "Two Dads" model. While gay couples raising kids exist, they are just one of many configurations.
 * The Romantic Husband: Two (or more) lovers raising a child.
 * The Platonic Co-Father: Two friends who are not lovers but signed a contract to raise a son together. They may live together or separately.
 * The Solo Father: A single man raising a son (very common).
 * The Network Family: A child raised by a "constellation" of adults—biological sire, legal guardian, and mentors.
 * The Compound: Multi-generational households where uncles, grandfathers, and cousins raise children communally.
Writing Tip: When introducing a character's parents, clarify the dynamic. "He was raised by his father and his father's business partner" implies a different dynamic than "He was raised by his fathers."
3. Romance and Sexuality
Arrhenos is a single-gender society, meaning all local romance is male/male.
 * No "Coming Out": There is no concept of "gay" or "straight" on Arrhenos because there is no alternative. A man loving a man is as unremarkable as gravity. Do not write stories about the "struggle" of homosexuality on Arrhenos; it doesn't exist.
 * Poly-Romance: Relationships involving three or more men are normal and legally recognized.
 * Asexuality/Aromanticism: Perfectly valid and integrated. Since reproduction is a bureaucratic choice, there is no pressure to have sex to "carry on the line."
4. Visuals and Technology
The Torc
 * Ubiquity: Almost every Arrhene citizen wears a torc (metal neck band).
 * Function: It is their ID, wallet, medical monitor, and communication device (via neural interface or holography).
 * Description: Use the torc in scenes. It glows when receiving data; characters touch it to authorize payments; it can be removed for privacy (a significant act).
 * Style: Plain steel (utilitarian), gold/gem-set (wealthy), or bioluminescent (artistic).
Environment
 * The 20-Hour Day: Remember the rhythm is faster. Midnight is 20:00. Midday is 10:00.
 * Botany: The trees are genetically adapted. Foliage is often purple, copper, or violet, not just green. Mention this to ground the reader.
 * Architecture: Green architecture ("The Moss" in Campottonì) and translucent materials are common.
5. Tonal Pitfalls to Avoid
1. The "Prison Planet" Vibe
Arrhenos is not a gritty, testosterone-fueled prison yard. It is a high-functioning, civilized republic known for banking, art, and philosophy.
 * Avoid: Excessive aggression, constant physical posturing, or "alpha male" tropes.
 * Do: Intellectual debate, complex etiquette, emotional vulnerability (it is safe to cry here).
2. The "Hidden Mother"
Do not give a character a "secret mother" or a "long-lost sister." This breaks the world-building. The Continuity Programme is absolute.
3. The "Gay Utopia" Simplification
While it is free of homophobia, it is not a utopia.
 * Class: There is wealth inequality (e.g., the wealthy of Brovdingonai vs. the transients of Verenstad).
 * Loneliness: In a society of 2 billion men, isolation is a major theme.
 * Politics: Bureaucracy is stifling. The "Axiomatic" vs. "Romantic" philosophical divides cause real conflict.
6. Location shorthand
Use the right city for the right vibe:
 * Campottonì: The Capital. Power, government, old money, "The Moss."
 * Landwick: Culture. Arts, theater, humidity, the "Tep" (bathhouse district).
 * Brovdingonai: Comfortable prosperity. Suburbs, finance, Jockey stadiums, families.
 * Verenstad/Gamma: The Spaceport. Transients, grit, trade, off-worlders, the Tether.
 * Zespol (The Nemora): Wilderness. Cold, research, isolation, nature.
7. Quick Checklist

 * [ ] Is the family structure specific? (Avoid defaulting to nuclear norms).
 * [ ] Is the Torc mentioned?
 * [ ] Is the day cycle correct (20 hours)?
 * [ ] Are the emotional stakes grounded in the setting (e.g., banking/merit/loneliness) rather than external prejudices (e.g., homophobia)?
 * [ ] If there is a romance, is it treated as the default norm?
