# Faster-Than-Light Travel

Alcubierre-Vishwanathan (AV) drives enable interstellar travel by manipulating spacetime geometry. A ship with such a drive travels within a moving pocket of flat space.

## The curvature limit

The field equations governing the formation of warp pockets undergo a phase transition as spacetime curvature increases, and stable pocket formation is impossible above a critical curvature threshold. Around the Sun this threshold occurs at approximately 30 AU, and warp cannot function closer than that.

The radius of the resulting "jump shell" scales with the cube root of the stellar mass: for a single star of mass `n M_☉`, the shell is to a first approximation a sphere of radius `r ≈ 30 × cbrt(n) AU`. Systems with multiple stars or very large gas giants can have complex exclusion geometries.

## Pocket characteristics

The time spent within a warp pocket depends on the relative velocities of the endpoints of the trajectory, and is on the order of hours within a typical stellar neighbourhood.

A warp pocket is causally disconnected from its interior. The ship cannot steer or communicate, so navigation is predictive: the destination system's position and the intervening gravitational influences must be accounted for before departure.

The Vishwanathan field configuration is only metastable. Early pockets collapsed catastrophically, destroying three research stations before adequate stabilisation protocols emerged. Failure of modern systems is rare but not unknown, and should leave the ship temporarily stranded rather than destroyed.

## Travel

Within a system's jump shell, ships travel by sublight drives—fusion torch, ion, solar sail. Outside the shell they travel between stars on AV drives.

An interstellar journey therefore has three stages:

- a sublight trek outwards to the jump shell of the home system (days to weeks)
- interstellar transit under the AV drive (hours)
- a sublight trek inwards from the jump shell of the destination system (days to weeks)

The sublight legs completely dominate travel time. A journey of forty light-years might take a week, of which virtually all is spent crawling to and from the jump shells at conventional speeds.

## Communication

There is no faster-than-light signalling independent of the AV drive. The fastest way to send information between stars is as cargo on a ship. Autonomous courier ships loiter around infrastructure nodes at the jump shells of major systems, transmitting and receiving data from the inner systems in light-speed packets.

The most efficient method is therefore to broadcast by radio from planetside or orbit to the ports at the jump shell, transport the data on a physical substrate at warp speed to another system's shell, and radio it in to the destination's inner system.

Messages may travel directly along well-trafficked lanes or make use of intermediate relay nodes for less-travelled routes. One-way communication times thus vary from hours to weeks, but are never fast enough for real-time conversation between systems.

## Infrastructure

In principle a ship can emerge from warp anywhere on the jump shell. In practice they emerge wherever there is existing infrastructure. Ships require updated maps, fuel, cargo transfer and maintenance, and these services accumulate at nodes—typically a handful per system, sometimes dozens in heavily trafficked space.

Major commercial nodes offering fuel, cargo, refits, housing and entertainment may house tens of thousands permanently, with transient populations several times larger. Military installations for sensors, intelligence and resupply may commingle with these or be kept deliberately separate.

## Jump gates

Civilisations with sufficient capital may construct jump gates on the jump shell. Gates lower engineering and economic limitations by projecting externally sustained warp fields and pre-calculating stable trajectories, which allows ships without their own AV drives to transit between stars. Control of a gate represents immense economic prosperity, military advantage and political leverage.

The alloy fez has material properties that drastically reduce gate construction time, resource requirements and maintenance costs, making gates economically viable for smaller or more remote systems. Access to fez thus affects which systems can afford to participate in the galactic economy.

## History

Miguel Alcubierre's 1994 paper demonstrated that general relativity permitted warp drive solutions which could carry a ship faster than light without local superluminal motion. Theoretical developments in the twenty-first and twenty-second centuries lowered the negative energy required by over a dozen orders of magnitude, and failed to make any progress towards implementing any negative energy density at all in practice, and interest in the problem dwindled.

The Bengaluru Breakthrough took place in 2488 C. Priya Vishwanathan's team at the Mysore Institute was studying topological defects in exotic vacuum states when one of their number realised that certain field configurations, which appeared to require impossible energy densities, were in fact accessible through careful state preparation.

The first artificial warp pocket, containing a single proton, was sustained for 0.0031 seconds in a deep-space laboratory on 7.2/4, 2488 C. The first crewed vessel, launched in 2511 C, carried two passengers in a space smaller than a wardrobe. Scaling to useful cargo capacity required another decade of field geometry refinements, and by 2520 C reliable interstellar travel had become possible.

Priya Vishwanathan died in 2534 C, forty-six years after her breakthrough.
