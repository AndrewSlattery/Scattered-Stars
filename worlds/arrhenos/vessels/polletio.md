# The Polletio

A mid-range freighter of the Arrhenos Merchant Fleet: 2,800 crew, ninety-seven years old with refits every decade, working the Hesperine Circuit.

The circuit runs Arrhenos to Tethys-Var, then three Mandate waypoints, then Korsen's Anchorage, and home by way of the Vega trade stations, with dozens of stops at minor ports and rec stations in between. A full circuit takes fourteen months.

Arrhene ships are gendered masculine, a linguistic quirk carried over from the generation ships—the *Polletio* himself, then. Crew talk about him as though he has opinions. He's running hot means the environmental systems are off; he doesn't like that cargo means the weight distribution feels wrong.

## Work

A standard day runs two half-shifts of three hours each, separated by at least six hours.

Engineering divides into Drive for the AV systems, Power for the fusion reactors and distribution, Structure for bulkheads and floors and hull, and Systems for everything else. Navigation keeps its own hours, clustered around jump calculations, and their mess has better coffee; everyone knows this and nobody knows why.

## Living aboard

Ordinary crew sleep in compartments of eighteen: three three-high stacks of cots, two men per cot, barely a metre wide. You learn your bunkmate's breathing, his smell, whether he kicks in dreams. Some men request transfers after a week, and others pair up for years. Senior crew get one man per cot and officers have actual cabins.

The wetrooms sprawl across Deck Four—sixty showers, a main pool that runs cold near the filtration intake, two properly hot soaking pools, steam rooms, a sauna, and private chambers with doors but no locks, the etiquette being a double knock and then a wait. There is a social geography to it: the port-side pool tends toward older crew, the steam room toward Drive engineers. Learning the map takes a few weeks.

Mess Hall One seats two hundred and feeds the crew in rolling waves. The food is real—hydroponics, protein vats, actual cooking—and the quality varies with the cook. Fresh supplies from a station stop transform the menu for weeks. Other messes range from smaller copies of One down to a few seats and a stationary food-truck arrangement.

There are three bars: the Canteen, which is official; the Hollow, a repurposed storage bay with a reputation; and a third with no name that moves. Engineering Lower runs a semi-authorised still with stronger booze than any of them.

## Port calls

The circuit puts the *Polletio* in port roughly every six weeks.

| Tethys-Var         | Three weeks loading fez, with half the crew on enforced downtime. Rough stations, and fights are common |
| Korsen's Anchorage | Medical services, entertainment, and a reputation for discretion                                        |
| Vega stations      | Expensive. Men blow their savings on luxuries they will regret                                          |
| Arrhenos           | A chance to go home, or to stay aboard. About a third choose the ship                                   |

The *Polletio* is not famous. He is not the largest ship in the Fleet, nor the fastest, nor on the most glamorous route. Some men serve a contract and leave; others stay for decades, carving out corners that become theirs. There is a word in Hesperine dialect, *vethallen*, roughly "the place that holds you"—neither home nor trap exactly, but the space you return to because returning has become what you do.

## Crew

Caden Orroway, 54 and looking forty, has been Chief Engineer for thirty-one years aboard and has turned down shore promotions twice, for reasons he keeps to himself. The engineering sections run smoothly under him, not through force of personality but through systems built over decades: training programmes, redundancies, a culture of competence. He eats alone more often than not, and men respect him without quite liking him, which seems to be the arrangement he prefers.

Fenno Blackwell, 26, does environmental systems maintenance officially and tends bar at the Hollow actually. Slight, with an easy manner that makes men talk, and what they tell him stays with him—a reputation carefully cultivated. The Hollow runs on his discretion as much as its smuggled spirits. He sends money home to his fathers in Landwick every pay cycle, enough that they have stopped asking when he is coming back.

Seoras Thain, 40, is cargo foreman; Vel Dorian works communications, having come up through Navigation. They met on a previous vessel and Vel transferred to the *Polletio* when Seoras moved up to foreman, and have been together nine years. They share a senior double-bunk modified with a curtain for the illusion of privacy. The relationship is romantic in a quiet, settled way, neither performed nor hidden nor explained: Seoras makes Vel coffee before his shift and Vel waits for Seoras after his. When they argue—about money, about family visits, about whether to renew contracts together—they do it in low voices, and then one of them goes to the wetrooms until the edge wears off. They have talked about leaving the merchant life and finding work planetside, and the conversations never go anywhere.
