# Little Arrhenos: the Aurelius City Enclave

Every Little Arrhenos is different, and the one in Aurelius City is the biggest, the richest, and the most argued-about—the capital, insofar as the diaspora has one, of the Arrhene abroad. It sits in eleven blocks of a great unglamorous working city on the Meridine continent of a Vega planet, where the [reconstruction](../external-relations.md) has been administered for twenty-three years, and it is what happens when the galaxy's most home-tethered diaspora stays anywhere long enough to stop calling it a posting.

## The Steamworks

The [bathhouse came first](../diaspora.md)—it nearly always does—built in 3843 C inside a war-damaged thermal plant the enclave's founders bought cheap and never stopped extending. Seven pools on three levels, the steam rooms, the cold plunge nobody admits to skipping, and the alcove galleries where a remarkable fraction of the reconstruction's actual business has been settled by men waist-deep in hot water while the formal meetings upstream ratified the results.

The Steamworks is the embassy, the club, and the hearth, and it is also the enclave's border crossing. Vegans are welcome, and a certain kind of Vegan—clerks from the Exchange, engineers from the reconstruction works, the odd minor noble entertained by the absence of protocol—has made it a habit, learning the etiquette of the double knock and discovering what it does to a negotiation to conduct it with nowhere to pin a medal. Women bathe here too; the pools sort by temperature, not by anything else, and to first-time Vegan visitors that is the strangest sentence in the district. To the regulars it stopped being interesting years ago, which is the most Arrhene outcome available.

## Ledger Row

The working spine: the [Consortium](../economy.md) house and its satellite firms, the arbitration chambers where Banking-War dockets are still being ground toward 3890, the [Merchant Fleet](../vessels/polletio.md) agents, the noodle counters and tailors and torc-technicians that grow wherever Arrhenes are paid. The senior men rotate home on contract cycles; the juniors dream of the transfer to Campottonì; and a stubborn residue of both keep *not leaving*, renewing contracts they swore were their last, because somewhere in the second decade the posting quietly became the life.

## The children

The enclave holds, at last count nobody official made, some hundreds of children with an Arrhene father—the daughters and sons of Vegan marriages, the adopted, the wards—raised half in the bath-house manner and half in Vega's, keeping the single pronoun at home and the gendered ones at school, and fluent past their fathers in both. The Steamworks runs a children's hour on rest-day mornings that would be unremarkable on Arrhenos and is, in Vega, a small standing scandal that everyone involved has agreed not to notice.

## People

**Orsandher Vell** (61) has run the Consortium house for eleven years—a posting that was the making of his career, exactly as the senior men promise, and that he has twice declined to leave. He chairs the Steamworks trust and hosts the Foundation Day feast.

**Maru** (14) is the daughter of a Fleet-agent father and a Vegan mother who manages an Exchange floor, raised in two grammars, unbeatable at the Steamworks children's hour. She has never seen Arrhenos. She can name the nine months faster than her father, in an accent that makes him laugh: Vega vowels on Campottonì words.

**Tekemi Osthard** (44) came out with the arbitration tribunals, fell for a Vegan woman, and stayed. He is the enclave's worked example, cited in both directions: proof that the leaving happens, and proof that the leavers do not stop being Arrhene—he still keeps Foundation Day, still argues Axiomatic ethics in the steam, and is teaching his daughter to swim in the Steamworks pool with the grim thoroughness of a man who learned in the Nemora's lakes.

## The texture of it

The enclave keeps the calendar twice over—[Foundation Day](../index.md) in the Steamworks, the Vega Gap days because the city shuts anyway—and its weddings have become a minor Aurelius City spectacle: the dry Arrhene contract signing, then a feast that has learned Vegan lighting design and never looked back. Vega's own reading of the district runs the familiar range—a curiosity, a convenience, an indispensable nuisance—with one local addition: Aurelius City, which watches the aristocracy from the outside just as the Arrhenes do, has decided the bankers are, in their strange way, *neighbours*, and defends them in bar arguments against out-of-towners with the proprietary air of a city defending its own oddity.

Whether Little Arrhenos is a piece of the homeworld or the first draft of something else is the enclave's own version of the [oldest diaspora question](../diaspora.md), and it divides along a line nobody planned: the posted men mostly know they are from Arrhenos, and the children mostly know they are from here.
