# The Nemora and Zespol

A wilderness band of seven million square kilometres stretching from the equator to the north polar regions, protected by planetary treaty since 2956 C.

It is not a single forest but a continuous corridor of preserved ecological zones: tropical rainforest at the equator, then temperate deciduous forest, coniferous zones, taiga, tundra, and northern ice.

## Ecology

The ecology is entirely Earth-descended, Arrhenos having had no native life when the colonists arrived. What exists now is careful introduction and adaptation over nearly a millennium: some species are unmodified transplants, others have been tweaked for Arrhenos conditions, and others again are genuine innovations for niches that never existed on Earth.

The result looks familiar and wrong in subtle ways. Trees grow taller in the reduced gravity, certain bird wingspans would be impossible under Earth conditions, the undergrowth is denser, and the fungal networks are more aggressive.

## Habitation

Most of the Nemora is empty of permanent habitation. The treaty allows around three hundred research stations scattered throughout, and seven cities, none larger than half a million.

Whether wilderness can be preserved while being lived in, and whether the research fundamentally alters what it is supposedly preserving, are questions nobody has good answers to and everybody has opinions about.

## Zespol

At 58°N, 178°W: the largest city in the Nemora, around 280,000 people, in the transition zone between temperate and coniferous forest. It grew from Research Complex Seven, established in 2987 C, and sits in a bowl valley where the Kiruna River widens into interconnected lakes.

The climate is genuinely cool. Summers reach 20°C, winters drop to −30°C, and snow lies five months of the year. Zespol's cold is part of its identity. Buildings are low, designed for thermal efficiency, and many are partially subterranean.

The economy runs in roughly three equal parts by quid value: direct research employment, services supporting researchers, and people who came for the Nemora itself—wilderness guides, artists working with natural materials, scholars on sabbatical, and craftsmen producing goods the old way.

By ten, most children have basic wilderness training: navigation, avoiding dangerous modified species such as the wolves and engineered bears, and storm survival. A fifteen-year-old from Zespol can maintain a fire, identify a hundred plants, navigate by stars, and handle a predator encounter, and might struggle with urban density and constant connectivity.

Founders' Night falls on Oscur 1/9, at midwinter: a festival centred on bonfires in the Kiruna Flats—actual wood fires, carefully permitted, using deadfall from the previous year. No holographic displays and no quantum-linked entertainment. Just fire and food and people and the cold dark pressing in from the wilderness beyond the city's lights.

Kasimir, of Elio Webb's network, teaches abstract mathematics here, in rooms overlooking the wilderness preserves. He came twelve years ago, finding Campottonì exhausting, and Zespol offered space, physical and psychological. Whether he will stay permanently is an open question. The city attracts people who are, in some fundamental way, apart from mainstream Arrhene life.
