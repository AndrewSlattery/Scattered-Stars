# Arrhenos: the physical model (sketch v1)

A first pass at why Arrhenos looks the way it does: plates, relief, winds, currents, and what those do to the coasts. It keeps the outlines agreed in [decisions.md](decisions.md) and adjusts them where the physics argued for it; every such change is listed at the end for review. The maps and the files that make them are in [sketch/](sketch/README.md).

Nothing here is canon yet. Plate, ocean and range names are working labels.

## The planet

| Radius          | About Earth's. Canon fixes it twice: Brovdingonai is 3,000 km south of Landwick across 27° of latitude, and the Nemora's 7 million km² is exactly a 10°-wide band from equator to pole on an Earth-sized sphere. |
| Gravity         | Lower than Earth's (canon: trees grow taller). The value is open. |
| Day             | 20 hours. |
| Year            | 365 or 366 days; the orbit was adjusted to fit the C calendar. |
| Axial tilt      | Assumed Earth-like, about 23°: Brovdingonai has proper seasons and Zespol's winters reach −30°C. Open. |
| Land            | About 31% of the surface; the agreed target was about 30%. |
| Poles           | Land and an ice sheet in the north; open sea in the south. |

Three consequences follow.

- **The faster spin narrows the tropical circulation a little.** The Hadley cells reach about 27° rather than Earth's 30°. So the trade winds, the subtropical deserts and the westerly storm belts all sit about 3° nearer the equator than their Earth counterparts: deserts centred near 20–27°, westerlies from 27° to about 57°.
- **Lower gravity lets mountains and volcanoes stand taller for the same strength of rock.** The Andean ranges peak at about 7,000 m, which is Andes height, so there is room to go higher if you want.
- **The two poles are unlike each other.** The ice sheet on the northern polar continent makes the north the colder hemisphere, which suits Zespol. The south has no polar landmass, so it has sea ice rather than an ice cap, and the southern westerlies blow almost unbroken round the planet.

## Plates

The layout is the remains of an old supercontinent coming apart, which gives both the matching coastlines and the variety between regions.

| Western Ocean      | Opened like the Atlantic, by a slow ridge splitting C and A from B·nw and E. Its coasts are passive margins with wide shelves. The worn Carmanto Hills on A and the uplands on E's east coast are two halves of one old mountain belt torn apart by the opening, as the Appalachians and the Scottish Highlands were. |
| Equatorial Ridge   | A drifts south, away from C, widening the sea between Port Magnus and the capital's continent. |
| C                  | Ringed by spreading ridges (Western, Equatorial, Northern Sea and Polar) and with no active margin of its own, much like Africa: an old, stable continent with worn mountains. |
| The Rift Sea       | B·nw is tearing away from B. Young, hot and deep, like the Red Sea crossed with the Gulf of California: long, straight, parallel coasts under fault-block walls, volcanic islands on its axis, hot springs. Its two shores would fit back together. |
| Boreal Trench      | The Central Ocean's floor dives north under B·ne. A volcanic arc forms the peninsula, with a deep back-arc gulf behind it, as Japan and the Sea of Japan do. Further east, the B·ne Cordillera. |
| Eastern Trench     | The Southern Ocean's floor dives under A's east coast (an Andes-type cordillera) and round the tail. Further north it passes under F and a chain of volcanic islands to D's south coast, like the arc from Sumatra through the Lesser Sundas. |
| Austral Trench     | The south polar floor dives under A's south coast, raising the Austral Cordillera. The ice-capped tail is where it meets the Eastern Trench. |
| Verenstad Trench   | Builds the volcanic spine of the narrow peninsula down to Verenstad, as with Central America. |
| Meridian Trench    | Builds E's Andean west coast. |
| Nemoran West Trench | Gives D a volcanic west coast. |
| Nemoran Fault      | The boundary between D and B is a strike-slip fault, like the San Andreas or New Zealand's Alpine Fault. The Nemoran Strait is a fault-guided channel: deep, narrow, earthquake-prone, with fierce tidal streams. |
| Old structures     | The Campottonì Gulf is a failed rift arm left over from the Western Ocean's opening (like the Gulf of Suez, or the North Sea's grabens). The Nemoran Suture is where two old blocks were welded, as along the Urals; its worn uplands run the length of the Nemora. |
| Landwick hotspot   | A mantle plume erupted the Hesperine flood basalts (think the Deccan Traps, from the plume that now sits under Réunion). A has since drifted south over it, leaving a line of worn volcanic hills running north to the plume's present position just south-east of Landwick, where the geothermal field is. |
| H hotspot          | A plume near the Northeast Rise, building H's big shield volcanoes on an oceanic plateau. Galápagos in setting, Iceland or Kerguelen in bulk. Lower gravity helps them stand so large. |

Plate motions on the map are schematic, and the only drift drawn is A's, because the hotspot story depends on it.

## Relief, by landmass

| A     | The Austral Cordillera along the south coast and the higher Eastern Cordillera down the east (peaks near 7,000 m) meet in the ice-capped tail. Campottonì's high plains are the worn shoulders of the old rift on both sides of the gulf. The Marchpane is a broad lowland basin inland of them. The Hesperine Plateau is a flood-basalt tableland at about 950 m; Brovdingonai sits on its east rim, above the foreland lowlands that run to the Eastern Cordillera. Landwick is on a delta, with worn volcanic hills to its south. The Carmanto Hills are old and rounded. |
| B     | A volcanic arc and a steep cordillera along B·ne's south coast. Older ranges run through B·ne's interior. The Rift Walls rise over the Rift Sea. Worn uplands follow the Nemora north to Zespol, whose bowl valley sits among them. Two large glacial lakes are proposed (the old map had a lake district). An ice sheet about 3 km thick covers the pole. |
| B·nw  | Rift walls on the west, a volcanic spine down the peninsula, glaciated uplands in the north, and old uplands along the Western Ocean coast. Verenstad lies just east of the volcanic spine, on its lee side. |
| C     | Worn, glaciated highlands in the north; uplands along both rifted coasts; an old shield in the southern lobe around Port Magnus (about 500 m). The inland sea fills a scoured basin in the middle. |
| D     | The worn Nemoran uplands down the middle (not one ridge but a staggered line of massifs); volcanic ranges on the west and south-west coasts. |
| E     | A high Andean cordillera along the west coast (to about 7,000 m); old worn uplands in the east, with open plains between. |
| Small | F has a volcanic spine; G is a mountainous sliver of continent; H's shields reach about 3,800 m; I is low and hilly. |

## Winds and currents

The trade winds blow toward the equator from about 27° in both hemispheres. The westerlies blow from about 27° to 57°, and are stronger in the south, where almost nothing stands in their way. The polar easterlies blow beyond that.

The ocean model turns those winds into currents. The main ones:

| Warm (running poleward)      | Down A's east coast (like the East Australian Current); up B·nw's east coast and across to C's west coast (like the Gulf Stream); up C's east coast into the Northern Sea, warming I; down E's east coast (like the Brazil Current); up D's east coast. |
| Cold (running toward the equator) | Along A's west coast north and south of the Campottonì Gulf (like the Benguela); along C's south-west coast (Canary); along E's west coast (Humboldt); down the Verenstad peninsula's west coast (California); along D's west coast. |
| Other                        | A cold subpolar current runs north up the tail's east side (like the Falklands Current). The west-wind drift circles the south with only A's tail in its way, and passes south of that. The equatorial currents run west. |

The model is deliberately simple: steady, wind-driven and depth-averaged. It gets the gyres and the warm and cold coasts right, but not the deep circulation, seasons or monsoons. The big tropical landmasses (A's north, B·ne, D) would have strong monsoons. Landwick's "sudden rain that steams off within the hour" fits one.

## What that does to the coasts

| Tidal gulf           | The Campottonì Gulf: an old rift, shallow and funnel-shaped, which is the recipe for very large tides (the Bay of Fundy, the Gulf of Khambhat). Expect mudflats and salt marsh, a tidal bore up the graben's river, and a harbour of locks and tidal basins. At 34°S on a west-facing gulf with a cold current offshore, the capital has a Mediterranean climate: dry summers and wet winters. |
| Cold-current deserts | A's west coast either side of the gulf, C's south-west, E's north-west, and the peninsula's west coast. These are fog deserts with smooth dune coasts and rich upwelling fisheries, like the Namib or Atacama. |
| Warm, humid coasts   | A's east coast from 15° to 40°S, B·nw's and E's east coasts, C's east coast: long beaches, barrier islands and lagoons. |
| Deltas and marsh     | Landwick's delta, on a warm, monsoonal coast, with brackish marsh as canon has it. Also the bay east of A's horn, and B·nw's delta gulf. |
| Fjords               | E's south-west coast (a Chilean or Norwegian coast: mountains plus westerlies plus past glaciers), the tail, G's west coast, C's north coast, and B·nw's north. |
| Storm coasts         | Windward coasts in the westerlies, such as A's far west around Carmanto: cliffs and heavy swell. |
| Rift coasts          | Both shores of the Rift Sea: straight, steep, narrow-shelved, young. |
| Inland sea           | C's sea: brackish, with small tides behind its narrow mouth. Its northern arm is skerry-strewn and freezes in winter. |
| Ice coasts           | The polar continent's shores. |
| The Nemoran Strait   | Fast tidal streams through a fault-bound channel bring up deep water. It is a rich marine environment, and it gives the Nemora an undersea reach. |

## If Arrhenos had no life before the colonists

Canon says Arrhenos had no native life. If so, its rocks record that absence. There would be no coal, oil or gas, and no chalk or reef limestone. Beaches would be quartz or black volcanic sand, and natural coral reefs, atolls and mangroves could not exist: any there are would have been planted less than a thousand years ago. Soils were made rather than inherited, and are thin and young except on the flood basalts and volcanic arcs, which weather fastest. That makes the Hesperine Plateau and the arc coasts naturally the most fertile ground. Ores would be magmatic and hydrothermal (arcs, old mountain belts) rather than sedimentary, so Carmanto's industry would stand on the metal ores of the old Carmanto belt and on fusion power, not coal.

These are suggestions for texture, not changes to canon. Whether the atmosphere was made breathable before the Fleet arrived is a separate question the text doesn't yet answer.

## Changes beyond the questionnaire, for review

1. **A's gulf** is narrower and straighter than before, because it is a rift. Its head is at the capital, and the old gulf's inner end is now a rift valley with a river running down to Campottonì.
2. **A's south coast and east coast** are active margins with high ranges. This replaces the old map's scattered mountains.
3. **A** comes out at about 52M km² after trims to the tail, the north arm and the north coast (the old map's A was 49M). **E** is reshaped to about 11M km², with a straight Andean west coast in place of its old bay.
4. **B·ne's south-facing gulf** is now a deep back-arc sea behind a volcanic arc peninsula, so that it isn't a third copy of the enclosed-gulf shape.
5. **B's polar coast** on the far side of the pole sits at about 81–82°N, so that the pole is land.
6. **C's inland sea** is enlarged (about 1.3M km²), with a second basin in the south. C's coasts have been pulled in a little.
7. **Landwick** sits on a delta.
8. **Brovdingonai** sits on the Hesperine Plateau's east rim.
9. **Two great lakes** are proposed in B, each about the size of Lake Superior.
10. **New small islands:** an island arc between F and D, eroded islets trailing west from H, one island each on the Western Ocean and Northern Sea ridges, and two in the Rift Sea.
11. **Five proposed spaceport sites** alongside canon Verenstad and Port Magnus: A's horn, F, H, and one either side of the Nemora on D.
12. **Land** now covers about 31% of the surface rather than 30%. The polar cap and the new C inland-sea basin account for most of the difference.

## Open questions

- Axial tilt and surface gravity. Both are assumed above; either changes mountain heights, ice and seasons.
- Moons. Landwick's tide-halls "dry twice a day" need a moon (or strong solar tides), and the size of the Campottonì Gulf's tides depends on it.
- Names: oceans, plates, ranges, the landmasses themselves.
- Whether to keep the lakes and the new islands.
- Zespol's bowl could be an old caldera or a worn impact structure. Either would explain the ring of uplands, and both are lore hooks.
