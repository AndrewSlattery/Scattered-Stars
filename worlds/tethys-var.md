# Tethys-Var

A moon of the gas giant Lophore-Tethys, in the Lophore system, supplying about 13.5% of the galaxy's fez—the largest single share anyone holds. It cannot be lived on, only worked. Permanent population runs to around 800,000 across Port Lophore and the stations, and rises to five or six million in season, on no notice.

## The bloom

Fez is metastable, and on Tethys-Var it forms metastably too. The moon rides the gas giant's tides and radiation belts, and somewhere in the interaction, by processes still incompletely modelled after four centuries of motivated study, veins of harvestable fez precipitate in the upper crust in irregular events the trade calls blooms.

A bloom announces itself on the survey nets with days or weeks of warning. It may run for a month or a year, and the fez in it begins decaying toward worthless base ore from the moment it forms. Hence the trade's vocabulary of agriculture applied to a poison rock: the fez must be harvested, ripe, fast, or lost.

Hence everything else. A bloom means five million workers arriving on chartered ships within weeks, and a lull means the moon shedding them just as fast. The impossibility of predicting either is why the fez price swings between one and fifteen million quid a kilogram, why the futures market attached to Tethys-Var is the most vicious in human space, and why the Cartel's fez desk would give a great deal to model the bloom cycle even a season ahead of the competition.

## Port Lophore

The permanent capital of the trade: a sprawling orbital complex above the moon, where harvesting licences are administered, berths are auctioned, futures are settled, and the harvest crews are hired, paid, entertained and separated from their pay. Below it the surface installations—hardened harvest stations, processing plants, the crawler fleets—stand ready at whatever strength the last bloom justified. Between blooms they run skeleton crews.

In season, Port Lophore is one of the roughest working environments in settled space, its population quintupled by men and women on short contracts and shorter tempers. The *Polletio* spends three weeks here every circuit loading fez, with half the crew on enforced downtime at any time, and the standing advice of the Arrhene Fleet to its men is to spend less than you have, drink less than you're offered, and be back aboard before third shift. Fights are common. The port's peace officers are Consortium employees and keep the Consortium's peace, which is the kind concerned chiefly with property.

## The Consortium

The Tethys Consortium holds two-thirds of the harvesting licences and has held them against all comers for nearly two centuries: a hard, unglamorous, locally rooted cartel of the old harvesting families and the operators they absorbed, run from Port Lophore by a licensing board whose seats are the most fought-over commercial property in the system.

Its power rests on the licences, on the infrastructure, since nobody else owns crawler fleets at scale, and on the survey nets, whose bloom data it shares by tier, late and expensive, with everyone it has decided to tolerate.

It is not a great power and does not want to be one. It wants the trade, whole, forever, and defends that with lawyers at Port Lophore, lobbying in every capital, and—out on the contested leases, where escalation is deniable—with means the lawyers do not discuss.

## The others

Cartel proxies work the minority licences and the seams, harvesting through subsidiaries and speculating through the futures desk, and competing notoriously with one another. Nowhere does the Cartel's internal arbitration referee more Cartel-against-Cartel disputes than in the Lophore system, where the left hand and the right regularly bid against each other for the same lease.

The Arrhene Merchant Fleet carries a good share of it: neutral hulls, honest scales, and no position in the futures market its clients are knifing each other over, which is why the factions that cannot bear to ship with one another's carriers will both ship with it. The Fleet's berths at Port Lophore are the calmest thing in the port, and the Consortium's licensing board and the Cartel's factors both, on occasion, hold meetings aboard Arrhene ships for the neutrality of the deck they stand on.

The independents run from licensed smallholders working marginal leases the Consortium does not want, down through the grey trade to the outright illegal: wildcat crews who drop onto unlicensed ground during a bloom, cut fast, and run before enforcement reaches them. In a good bloom the wildcats are a nuisance. In a marginal one, when every tonne matters, the shooting starts.

## The shadow

Every fortune on Tethys-Var is a bet that fez stays necessary. The Republic's laboratory demonstration of fez-free gate operation was trivial on its own terms, and the moon heard about it the way a company town hears about a factory closure announced for someday: officially dismissed, universally discussed, and quietly priced into everything. The Consortium funds its rebuttals, the Cartel positions for either outcome, and the harvest crews work the blooms while the blooms are worth working.
