# Tethys-Var

The most valuable dirt in human space is a moon that cannot be lived on, only worked. Tethys-Var, circling a gas giant in the Lophore system, supplies about an eighth of the galaxy's [fez](setting/technology/fez.md)—the largest single share anyone holds—and everything about the place follows from one fact: the fez comes when it comes, not when anyone wants it. Nobody schedules Tethys-Var. Tethys-Var schedules everybody, and several of the galaxy's most patient institutions have organised themselves around its moods.

## The bloom

Fez is metastable—that is the whole point of it—and on Tethys-Var it *forms* metastably too. The moon rides the gas giant's tides and radiation belts, and somewhere in the interaction, by processes that remain incompletely modelled after four centuries of exceedingly motivated study, veins of harvestable fez precipitate in the upper crust in irregular events the trade calls **blooms**. A bloom announces itself on the survey nets with days or weeks of warning; it may run for a month or a year; and the fez in it begins decaying toward worthless base ore from the moment it forms. Hence the trade's whole vocabulary of agriculture applied to a poison rock: the fez must be *harvested*, ripe, fast, or lost.

Hence also everything else. A bloom means five million workers arriving on chartered ships within weeks; a lull means the moon shedding them just as fast; and the impossibility of predicting either is why the fez price swings between one and fifteen million quid a kilogram, why the futures market attached to Tethys-Var is the most vicious in human space, and why the [Cartel's fez desk](powers/sable-cartel/operations.md) would give a great deal—and has tried more than once, by means the Consortium prosecutes when it can catch them—to model the bloom cycle even a season ahead of the competition.

## Port Lophore

The permanent capital of the trade: a sprawling orbital complex above the moon, where the harvesting licences are administered, the berths are auctioned, the futures are settled, and the harvest crews are hired, paid, entertained, and separated from their pay. Below it, the surface installations—hardened harvest stations, processing plants, the crawler fleets—stand ready at whatever strength the last bloom justified; between blooms they run skeleton crews and the port above runs on maintenance, speculation, and memory.

In season, Port Lophore is one of the roughest working environments in settled space—a boomtown in orbit, its population quintupled by men and women on short contracts and shorter tempers—and its dock rows are the reason merchant crews speak of the place the way they do. The [*Polletio*](worlds/arrhenos/vessels/polletio.md) spends three weeks here every circuit loading fez, with half the crew on enforced downtime at any time, and the standing advice of the Arrhene Fleet to its men is a masterpiece of understatement: *spend less than you have, drink less than you're offered, and be back aboard before third shift.* Fights are common. The port's peace officers are Consortium employees, and keep the Consortium's peace, which is the kind concerned chiefly with property.

## The Consortium

The **Tethys Consortium** holds two-thirds of the harvesting licences and has held them, against all comers, for nearly two centuries: a hard, unglamorous, locally-rooted cartel of the old harvesting families and the operators they absorbed, run from Port Lophore by a licensing board whose seats are the most fought-over commercial property in the system. The Consortium's power rests on three things: the licences, the infrastructure (nobody else owns crawler fleets at scale), and the survey nets, whose bloom data it shares by tier, late and expensive, with everyone it has decided to tolerate.

It is not a great power and does not want to be one. It wants the trade, whole, forever, and it defends that want with lawyers at Port Lophore, lobbying in every capital, and—out on the contested leases, where escalation is deniable—with means the lawyers do not discuss. The [gate-efficiency research](powers/neo-solar-republic/technology.md) in Sol is the first threat in its history it cannot out-litigate, and the Consortium's funding of rebuttal research is public, energetic, and, its own scientists privately concede, losing.

## The others

**Cartel proxies** work the minority licences and the seams—harvesting through subsidiaries, speculating through the futures desk, and competing, notoriously, *with one another*: nowhere does the Cartel's internal arbitration referee more Cartel-against-Cartel disputes than in the Lophore system, where the left hand and the right hand regularly bid against each other for the same lease and the legend of coordination goes to die. See [the Sable Cartel](powers/sable-cartel/operations.md).

**The Arrhene Merchant Fleet** does the carrying: neutral hulls, honest scales, and no position in the futures market its clients are knifing each other over—which is precisely why every faction ships with it. The [Fleet's](worlds/arrhenos/vessels/polletio.md) berths at Port Lophore are the calmest thing in the port, and the Consortium's licensing board and the Cartel's factors both, on occasion, hold meetings aboard Arrhene ships for the neutrality of the deck they stand on.

**The independents** run from licensed smallholders—families working marginal leases the Consortium doesn't want—down through the grey trade to the outright illegal: wildcat crews who drop onto unlicensed ground during a bloom, cut fast, and run before the Consortium's enforcement reaches them. In a good bloom the wildcats are a nuisance; in a marginal one, when every tonne matters, the shooting starts, and the territorial disputes the trade press reports as "friction" are, on the ground, small ugly wars between people racing a decay curve.

## The shadow over everything

Every fortune on Tethys-Var is a bet that fez stays necessary. The Republic's laboratory demonstration of fez-free gate operation was, on its own terms, trivial—three per cent, decades from application—and the moon heard about it the way a company town hears about a factory closure announced for someday: officially dismissed, universally discussed, and quietly priced into everything. The Consortium funds its rebuttals; the Cartel positions for either outcome, as ever; and the harvest crews, who own no licences and hold no futures, work the blooms while the blooms are worth working. On Tethys-Var that has always been the whole of wisdom anyway. The fez decays. Cut fast.
