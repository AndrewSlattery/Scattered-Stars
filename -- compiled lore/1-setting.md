# The C Calendar

The C Calendar is a standard timekeeping system across human space. Planets that have not been synchronised to the standard year usually maintain parallel local calendars of their own.

| second | base unit      | 0.864 SI seconds |
| minute | 100 seconds    | 86.4 SI seconds  |
| hour   | 50 minutes     | 72 SI minutes    |
| day    | 20 hours       | 1 SI day         |
| week   | 10 days        |                  |
| month  | 4 weeks        |                  |
| year   | 9 months + Gap |                  |

On worlds and stations synchronised to the C day, dawn occurs around the 5th hour, midday at the 10th hour, evening near the 15th hour, and midnight at the 20th hour.

The standard date format is recorded as `year C month.week/day`. For example, 3540 C 4.2/7 designates year 3540, the 4th month, the 2nd week, and the 7th day.

## The Gap

The Gap is a period of five or six days inserted between the final month of the old year and the first month of the new year. These days are formatted as `yyyy C Gap day` (e.g. 3862 C Gap 4).

Regional traditions and superstitions have accumulated around the Gap days. More information on the first three of the below examples can be found in the relevant power's files.

| Gap 1–3 | Mandate      | Misrule
| Gap 2   | Hv Ascendant | Supply Assay
| Gap 4   | Arrhenos     | Foundation Day
| Gap 6   | multiple     | auspicious for risky ventures, contracts, and marriages

# The Scattered Stars: Overview

3862 C | Human Diaspora

Beginning in 2488 C, humanity began to expand across the galaxy. Today, the diaspora encompasses over 4,000 inhabited star systems, roughly 10,000 terrestrial worlds, and upwards of 50,000 artificial habitats. Over a million stellar bodies have been surveyed and catalogued.

The fundamental constraints on FTL travel have made a centralized galactic government impossible. There have been no serious attempts at unification since the Cascade of 2756 C, when 847 worlds simultaneously declared independence from their Earth-based metropole authorities within a single year.

## The shape of human space

Human space is a lumpy, ragged region roughly a thousand light-years across at its widest. Of the million-odd catalogued stellar bodies, some four thousand systems are inhabited, and between any two settled systems lie dozens with nothing more than a survey beacon and a name.

## Culture and society

Linguistic fragmentation is common: planets with multiple isolated settlements often maintain dozens of unique local languages. A lingua franca known simply as "trade tongue" has developed; while it is almost never spoken as a native language, most who deal across star systems learn it (and most who don't don't).

Political structures vary enormously, from imperial hierarchy and corporate feudalism to direct democracies and frontier anarchy.

## The Great Powers

While thousands of independent nations exist, the geopolitical landscape is dominated by seven Great Powers who have successfully leveraged infrastructure, commerce, or military might to project influence across the light-years:

| The Mandate            | A sprawling, imperial hegemony centred on Tiamming.                 |
| Frontier Freeholds     | A fiercely decentralised coalition of border territories.           |
| Elysian Collective     | Terraforming masters who command high prices for their technology.  |
| Sable Cartel           | A distributed commercial oligarchy holding vital FTL infrastructure.|
| Neo-Solar Republic     | Transhuman utopianists holding the ancestral solar system.          |
| Vega Commercial Throne | A feudal society built on media, culture, and luxury goods.         |
| Drift Communities      | Nomadic, space-station societies without fixed infrastructure.      |

# Trade and Commerce

## Currency

Quid (ϟ): the quid is a trade currency honoured by every major power and by most polities that do serious interstellar business. It is available both in secure digital formats and as physical "pucks" for use in systems with unstable data infrastructure or for untraceable transactions.

Regional currencies: locally, human space runs on thousands of other standards. Great powers issue their own sovereign currencies; minor polities and frontier systems are typically awash in multiple overlapping monetary standards—company scrip, commodity notes, local fiat. Exchange services a ubiquitous and highly profitable necessity at all major trade hubs.

# Human Enhancement Technologies

Medical and cybernetic advancements have altered the human condition, though the adoption of these technologies varies wildly. Life expectancy with optimal acces to medical technology runs to approximately 200 years barring accidents, violence, or catastrophic illness, and the aging process has been dramatically slowed: even the non-augmented barely age visibly past their fortieth year.

## Enhancement eXtent (EX) Scale

Enhancements may be categorised using the EX Scale.

| EX 1.0     | baseline           | certain neural implants for communication and data access are considered routine |
| EX 1.1–1.5 | medical treatment  | synthetic organs, genetic therapies, age-related treatments |
| EX 1.6–2.0 | minor augmentation | enhanced senses, reinforced bones, improved endurance |
| EX 2.1–3.0 | major augmentation | extreme height modifications, density alterations for high- or low-gravity environments, redundant organs, enhanced musculature |
| EX 3.1–4.0 | reconstruction     | classification as a cyborg; involves major changes to the fundamental body plan| 
| EX 4.0+    | post-human         | radical morphological departures from the baseline human form | 

The majority of polities maintain an average population EX rating between 1.0 and 2.0. The Neo-Solar Republic is a notable exception, where heavily modified cyborg citizens are commonplace. Conversely, several worlds heavily restrict or entirely ban enhancements due to deeply held cultural or religious convictions.

# Metastable Iron-Zinc-Uranium Tennesside (FeZnUTs)


Metastable iron-zinc-uranium tennessite (known variously as "feznuts", "fez" or  "meta") is a highly prized and exceptionally rare material whose primary application is in the construction and maintenance of jump gates. Although not strictly necessary for their construction, fez drastically reduces their construction time, resource requirements, and ongoing maintenance costs.

The majority of fez is gathered from thousands of scattered sources, none of which accounts for more than 0.1% of the market share. However, a few major sources hold outsized strategic value and supply non-negligible portions of the total annual harvest, including:
| Tethys-Var  | ~13.5% |
| Nagara Deep | ~2%    |
| Prell       | ~0.5%  |

The Tethys Consortium exercises market control by holding 67% of the harvesting licences on Tethys-Var. Other players in the fez market include the Arrhenos merchant fleets, futures divisions associated with the Sable Cartel, and numerous independent harvesters running largely illegal operations.

## `setting/technology/ftl.md`

# Faster-Than-Light Travel

Alcubierre-Vishwanathan (AV) drives enable interstellar travel by manipulating spacetime geometry. A ship with such a drive travels within a moving pocket of flat space.

## The curvature limit

The field equations governing the formation of warp pockets undergo a phase transition as spacetime curvature increases, and stable pocket formation is impossible above a critical curvature threshold. Around the Sun this threshold occurs at approximately 30 AU, and warp cannot function closer than that.

The radius of the resulting "jump shell" scales with the cube root of the stellar mass: for a single star of mass `n M_☉`, the shell is to a first approximation a sphere of radius `r ≈ 30 × cbrt(n) AU`. Systems with multiple stars or very large gas giants can have complex exclusion geometries.

## Pocket characteristics

The time spent within a warp pocket depends on the relative velocities of the endpoints of the trajectory, and is on the order of hours within a typical stellar neighbourhood.

A warp pocket is causally disconnected from its interior. The ship cannot steer or communicate, so navigation is predictive: the destination system's position and the intervening gravitational influences must be accounted for before departure.

The Vishwanathan field configuration is only metastable. Early pockets collapsed catastrophically, destroying three research stations before adequate stabilisation protocols emerged. Failure of modern systems is rare but not unknown, and should leave the ship temporarily stranded rather than destroyed.

## Travel

Within a system's jump shell, ships travel by sublight drives—fusion torch, ion, solar sail. Outside the shell they travel between stars on AV drives.

An interstellar journey therefore has three stages:

- a sublight trek outwards to the jump shell of the home system (days to weeks)
- interstellar transit under the AV drive (hours)
- a sublight trek inwards from the jump shell of the destination system (days to weeks)

The sublight legs completely dominate travel time. A journey of forty light-years might take a week, of which virtually all is spent crawling to and from the jump shells at conventional speeds.

## Communication

There is no faster-than-light signalling independent of the AV drive. The fastest way to send information between stars is as cargo on a ship. Autonomous courier ships loiter around infrastructure nodes at the jump shells of major systems, transmitting and receiving data from the inner systems in light-speed packets.

The most efficient method is therefore to broadcast by radio from planetside or orbit to the ports at the jump shell, transport the data on a physical substrate at warp speed to another system's shell, and radio it in to the destination's inner system.

Messages may travel directly along well-trafficked lanes or make use of intermediate relay nodes for less-travelled routes. One-way communication times thus vary from hours to weeks, but are never fast enough for real-time conversation between systems.

## Infrastructure

In principle a ship can emerge from warp anywhere on the jump shell. In practice they emerge wherever there is existing infrastructure. Ships require updated maps, fuel, cargo transfer and maintenance, and these services accumulate at nodes—typically a handful per system, sometimes dozens in heavily trafficked space.

Major commercial nodes offering fuel, cargo, refits, housing and entertainment may house tens of thousands permanently, with transient populations several times larger. Military installations for sensors, intelligence and resupply may commingle with these or be kept deliberately separate.

## Jump gates

Civilisations with sufficient capital may construct jump gates on the jump shell. Gates lower engineering and economic limitations by projecting externally sustained warp fields and pre-calculating stable trajectories, which allows ships without their own AV drives to transit between stars. Control of a gate represents immense economic prosperity, military advantage and political leverage.

The alloy fez has material properties that drastically reduce gate construction time, resource requirements and maintenance costs, making gates economically viable for smaller or more remote systems. Access to fez thus affects which systems can afford to participate in the galactic economy.

## History

Miguel Alcubierre's 1994 paper demonstrated that general relativity permitted warp drive solutions which could carry a ship faster than light without local superluminal motion. Theoretical developments in the twenty-first and twenty-second centuries lowered the negative energy required by over a dozen orders of magnitude, and failed to make any progress towards implementing any negative energy density at all in practice, and interest in the problem dwindled.

The Bengaluru Breakthrough took place in 2488 C. Priya Vishwanathan's team at the Mysore Institute was studying topological defects in exotic vacuum states when one of their number realised that certain field configurations, which appeared to require impossible energy densities, were in fact accessible through careful state preparation.

The first artificial warp pocket, containing a single proton, was sustained for 0.0031 seconds in a deep-space laboratory on 7.2/4, 2488 C. The first crewed vessel, launched in 2511 C, carried two passengers in a space smaller than a wardrobe. Scaling to useful cargo capacity required another decade of field geometry refinements, and by 2520 C reliable interstellar travel had become possible.

Priya Vishwanathan died in 2534 C, forty-six years after her breakthrough.

# Terraforming Methodologies

The following descriptors classify the human habitation of various systems.

| Terrene | Full biosphere compatibility. The atmosphere is breathable, the ecosystem is sustainable, and no special life-support equipment is required. |
| Outdoor | A survivable atmosphere and a sustainable ecosystem. Long-term habitation is possible with only minor technological accommodations. |
| Dome | The surface is survivable only for short periods. The vast majority of activity and habitation is confined to enclosed, climate-controlled structures, including domes, sealed buildings, and underground complexes. |
| Suit | Personal atmosphere suits are strictly required at all times outside of small, heavily maintained habitats. Surface activity is extremely limited and highly resource-intensive. |
| Station | Planetary habitation is entirely impossible or bypassed. The population lives exclusively in fully constructed artificial habitats, such as orbital stations, asteroid installations, and worldships. |

Fully transitioning a hostile planet to Terrene or Outdoor status requires specialised ecological engineering.
