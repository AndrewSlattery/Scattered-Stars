# Appendix B: Diplomatic Protocols

## Contact procedures

There is no universal protocol for establishing relations between polities, but the corridor conventions are old and widely imitated. A first approach travels as a letter of introduction: a physical or quantum-sealed credential carried by courier, verifiable offline, naming the sending authority, the bearer's powers, and by long convention the sender's arbitration house of record. A polity that names no arbitrator is understood to be signalling either isolation or menace, and is received accordingly.

Off the corridors, practice varies without limit. Some polities receive envoys with centuries of accumulated ceremony, some auction the audience, and some have not received one in living memory and must look up what to do.

## Trade agreements

Common frameworks circulate along the trade routes, copied, localised and mutated with distance. Half a dozen lineages dominate: the Mandate's imperial commercial code wherever the Mandate can insist, Nexus paper on the frontier, house compacts in Vega space, Khivan forms across the coreward Circuit, the Weave's own conventions spinward, and the arbitration boilerplate of the neutral houses, of which the Arrhene is the oldest. Which lineage a contract is drawn in says more about where it was signed than about what it does.

## Declarations of war

Formal procedures exist and are inconsistently followed. The corridor convention—notice of grievance, a stated interval for satisfaction, declaration by courier to all affected neutral parties—is honoured most reliably by the great powers, who find that wars conducted by the forms are easier to end by them.

## Neutral ground

Certain grounds are held neutral by convention stronger than any treaty: the Drift havens, under the great havens' welcome codes; Korsen's Anchorage, where whatever your quarrel you do not pursue it here; the chambers of the neutral banking and arbitration houses, under whatever rules their clients have signed to; and the diplomatic spaces maintained bilaterally at hubs and border stations.

Violating recognised neutral ground is among the few acts that reliably unites everyone else against the violator, which is the only enforcement most of these grounds have.

# Appendix E: Open Questions

Three matters for which no settled account exists.

## The Empty Quarter Event

In 3644 C, over a period of at most some months, a coreward-spinward volume containing eleven catalogued minor systems—none populous, all inhabited—stopped answering. Couriers found the infrastructure intact and unattended: stations holding orbit, beacons cycling, no wreckage, no remains, no sign of departure and nothing departed to. The combined population of record was under forty million. No authority claimed the region before the event and none has resettled it since.

The systems' last outbound dispatches were ordinary. The first courier accounts disagree with one another in ways that later copying has worsened. The volume remains catalogued and is avoided by routing convention rather than by any regulation, and is periodically entered by salvage crews and researchers, most of whom return having found nothing.

Claims made without verifiable evidence include a terraforming failure of a novel class, a plague with perfect manners, a mass migration concealed for reasons unknown, and a Cartel operation, which the Cartel has characteristically never denied. The Drift havens will not schedule stops there and do not say why. The Mandate's records on the event are thinner than its records on anything else of comparable scale, which has been noticed.

## Kepler-442

Imperial Mandate forces deployed to the Kepler-442 system four years ago. Purpose and status classified. That is the entirety of the official record, and the classification has now held longer than such things usually hold, through a war on the trailing frontier that has stretched the same fleet thin everywhere else.

The system is unremarkable in every catalogue that predates the deployment. Analysts note only that the volumes of space the Mandate garrisons are, without other exception, either valuable or contested, and that Kepler-442 is on no published account either.

## The Lazarus Anomalies

On three documented occasions, Elysian Collective assessment teams surveying failed terraforming have found the remains of terraforming that failed differently: atmospheric chemistry wrong for every known method, geological work following no logic in the guild's Archive, biological remnants descending from no lineage the Conservatory holds. All three sites predate reliable records.

The Collective's official posture is cautious interest, its Seventh Grandmaster has made the sites a personal priority, and the guild that knows more about the making of worlds than anyone alive has examined them closely and does not know what it is looking at.

## Connection

These three could be arranged into a story, and the arrangement is a minor literary genre in its own right, whose variants contradict one another comprehensively. No known evidence connects the Empty Quarter, Kepler-442 and the Anomaly sites, and none rules a connection out.

# Appendix C: Navigation Data

## Jump infrastructure catalogues

Jump shell infrastructure nodes are catalogued and maintained by various authorities, which is to say by no single one. The corridor hubs are documented to the metre in a dozen rival catalogues; a frontier shell may be documented by one line in a prospector's log, decades stale. Prudent navigators buy at least two catalogues and believe neither entirely.

## Reference distances

Fixed interstellar distances matter less than they seem, the interstellar leg being effectively instantaneous, but they govern radio lag, and radio lag governs everything else.

| Sol to Vega                      | ~25 ly  | ~25 years by radio  |
| Tiamming to the Vega system      | ~210 ly | ~210 years by radio |
| A Sol-like star's jump shell     | ~30 AU  | ~4 hours by radio   |

## Pirate activity

Hotspots shift regularly. The Sable Cartel's insurance division maintains the most accurate data, available to clients—a fact that is itself a datum.

## Trade routes

The Golden Circuit carries electronics, fez and luxury goods. The Long Haul carries raw materials and salvage from the frontier regions. The Power Highways carry art, education and tourism between the major powers.

The Hesperine Circuit, the *Polletio*'s route, runs Arrhenos to Tethys-Var to the Mandate waypoints to Korsen's Anchorage to the Vega stations and home, with dozens of optional stops between each. Around fourteen months to a circuit.
