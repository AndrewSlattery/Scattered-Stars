# Appendix B: Diplomatic Protocols

## Contact Procedures

There is no universal protocol for establishing relations between polities—with three to four thousand of them, there could not be—but the corridor conventions are old and widely imitated. A first approach travels as a **letter of introduction**: a physical or quantum-sealed credential carried by courier, verifiable offline (nothing can be confirmed by a timely round-trip), naming the sending authority, the bearer's powers, and—by long convention—the sender's arbitration house of record. A polity that names no arbitrator is understood to be signalling either isolation or menace, and is received accordingly.

Off the corridors, practice varies without limit: some polities receive envoys with centuries of accumulated ceremony, some auction the audience, and some have not received one in living memory and must look up what to do.

## Trade Agreement Templates

Common frameworks for commercial treaties circulate along the trade routes, copied, localised, and mutated with distance. The **Arrhenos Financial Consortium's standard forms** are the prestige lineage—used verbatim in high-stakes cross-power work, because a contract in a form every arbiter knows is a contract that survives its signatories' quarrels—but most regional trade runs on local descendants of them, or on frameworks that owe Arrhenos nothing: Nexus paper on the frontier, house compacts in Vega space, the Mandate's Imperial commercial code wherever the Mandate can insist.

## War Declaration

Formal procedures exist and are inconsistently followed. The corridor convention—notice of grievance, a stated interval for satisfaction, declaration by courier to all affected neutral parties—is honoured most reliably by the great powers, who find that wars conducted by the forms are easier to end by them. The Cascade of 2756 C demonstrated the limits of diplomatic norms; the Andromedan Banking War (3834–39 C) demonstrated the modern failure mode, in which the shooting is well advanced before anyone concedes that what is occurring is a war.

## Neutral Zone Regulations

Certain grounds are held neutral by convention stronger than any treaty, because every party profits from their neutrality:

- **Arrhenos** (financial neutrality; arbitration under the Consortium's rules)
- **Drift havens** (neutral meeting grounds; the great havens' welcome codes)
- **Korsen's Anchorage** (the frontier's commons—*whatever your quarrel, you don't pursue it here*)
- **Designated diplomatic spaces** maintained bilaterally at hubs and border stations

Violating recognised neutral ground is among the few acts that reliably unites everyone else against the violator, which is the only enforcement most of these grounds have, and mostly the only enforcement they need.
