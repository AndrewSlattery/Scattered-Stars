# Appendix C: Navigation Data

## Jump Infrastructure Catalogues

AV drives require flat spacetime. Jump shell infrastructure nodes are catalogued and maintained by various authorities—which is to say, by no single one. The corridor hubs are documented to the metre in a dozen rival catalogues; a frontier shell may be documented by one line in a prospector's log, decades stale. Prudent navigators buy at least two catalogues and believe neither entirely. (For the physics and the node economics, see [FTL: Infrastructure](../setting/technology/ftl/infrastructure.md); for the geography they hang on, see [The Shape of Human Space](../setting/geography.md).)

## Reference Distances

Fixed interstellar distances matter less than they seem—the interstellar leg is effectively instantaneous—but they govern radio lag, and radio lag governs everything else.

| Route | Distance | Radio (one-way) | Express dispatch |
|-------|----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Arrhenos ↔ Tiamming | ~180 ly | ~180 years | ~4–6 days |
| Any planet ↔ its own jump shell | ~300 AU | ~1.7 days | — |

## Pirate Activity Maps

Current hotspots shift regularly. The Sable Cartel's insurance division maintains the most accurate data, available to clients—a fact that is itself a datum, and one the clients prefer not to dwell on.

## Trade Route Timetables

### Major Routes

- **The Golden Circuit**: Electronics, fez, luxury goods
- **The Long Haul**: Raw materials, salvage from frontier regions
- **Power Highways**: Art, education, tourism between major powers

### The Hesperine Circuit (Polletio route)

Arrhenos → Tethys-Var → Mandate waypoints → Korsen's Anchorage → Vega stations → Arrhenos
(alongside dozens of optional stops between each of the above)
Duration: ~14 months
