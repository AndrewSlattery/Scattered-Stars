# Appendix E: Open Questions

Three matters recur in the source material for which this compilation can offer no settled account. They are recorded here as they stand—claims, counter-claims, and the silence between them. This office declines to speculate, and notes that everyone else does not.

## The Empty Quarter Event (3644 C)

In 3644 C, over a period of at most some months, a coreward-spinward volume containing eleven catalogued minor systems—none populous, all inhabited—stopped answering. Couriers found infrastructure intact and unattended: stations holding orbit, beacons cycling, no wreckage, no remains, no sign of departure and nothing departed *to*. The combined population of record was under forty million. No authority claimed the region before the event; none has resettled it since.

What is documented: the systems' last outbound dispatches were ordinary. The first courier accounts disagree with one another in ways that later copying has worsened. The volume—since known as the **Empty Quarter**—remains catalogued, avoided by routing convention rather than by any regulation, and is periodically entered by salvage crews and researchers, most of whom return, having found nothing, which is itself the finding.

What is claimed, by various parties, with no evidence this office can verify: a terraforming failure of a novel class; a plague with perfect manners; a mass migration concealed for reasons unknown; a Cartel operation (the Cartel, characteristically, has never denied it); and every stranger thing a thousand years of retelling can grow. The Drift havens will not schedule stops there, and do not say why. The Mandate's records on the event are thinner than the Mandate's records on anything else of comparable scale, which has been noticed.

## Kepler-442 (3858 C – present)

Imperial Mandate forces deployed to the Kepler-442 system four years ago. Purpose and status: classified. That is the entirety of the official record, and the classification has now held longer than such things usually hold, through a war on the trailing frontier that has stretched the same fleet thin everywhere else. Whatever the Mandate is doing at Kepler-442, it considers the doing worth ships it demonstrably needs at Yansieve.

The system itself is unremarkable in every catalogue that predates the deployment. Analysts note only this: the volumes of space the Mandate garrisons are, without other exception, either valuable or contested, and Kepler-442 is on no published account either.

## The Lazarus Anomalies

On three documented occasions, Elysian Collective assessment teams surveying failed terraforming have found the remains of terraforming that failed *differently*—atmospheric chemistry wrong for every known method, geological work following no logic in the guild's Archive, biological remnants descending from no lineage the Conservatory holds. All three sites predate reliable records. The Collective's official posture is cautious interest; its Seventh Grandmaster has made the sites a personal priority; and the guild that knows more about the making of worlds than anyone alive has examined them closely and does not know what it is looking at. (See [The Lazarus Project](../powers/elysian-collective/lazarus-project.md).)

## A note on connection

It will not have escaped the reader that these three entries could be arranged into a story. It has not escaped anyone; the arrangement is a minor literary genre in its own right, and the variants contradict one another comprehensively. No evidence known to this office connects the Empty Quarter, Kepler-442, and the Anomaly sites. No evidence known to this office rules a connection out. The reader is invited to hold both sentences at once, which is the ordinary condition of knowledge in human space.
