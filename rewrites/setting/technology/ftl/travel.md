# FTL: Travel and Navigation

Within a system's jump shell, ships travel by sublight drives (fusion torch, ion, solar sail). Outside the shell, ships travel between stars using AV drives to create warp pockets.

If a system has a single star of mass `n M_☉`, the jump shell is (to a first approximation) a sphere with radius `r ≈ 30 × cbrt(n) AU`.

## Transit Patterns

A interstellar journey between star systems has three stages:
- sublight trek outwards to the jump shell of the home system (days to weeks)
- interstellar transit using the AV drive (hours)
- sublight trek inwards from the jump shell of the destination system (days to weeks)

The sublight legs completely dominate travel time. A journey of forty light-years might take a week, of which virtually all the time is spent crawling to and from jump shells at conventional speeds.

## Communication

The most efficient way to broadcast information is by radio from planetside or orbit to ports at the jump shell, then transporting the data on a physical substrate at warp speed to another system's shell, and then radioing into the destination's inner system. Lag between star systems is thus at least on the order of hours or up to a couple of days, assuming direct broadcast. Indirect communication via intermediate nodes can take days to weeks.
