# FTL: History

Miguel Alcubierre's 1994 paper demonstrated that general relativity permitted "warp drive" solutions which could carry a ship faster than light without local superluminal motion. Theoretical developements in the twenty-first and twenty-second centuries lowered the negative energy required by over a dozen orders of magnitude, but failed to make any progress towards implementing any negative energy density at all in practice, and interest in the problem dwindled.

The Bengaluru Breakthrough took place in 2488 C. Priya Vishwanathan's team at the Mysore Institute was studying topological defects in exotic vacuum states when one of their number realised that certain field configurations (which appeared to require impossible energy densities) were in fact accessible through careful state preparation.

The first artificial warp pocket, containing a single proton, was sustained for 0.0031 seconds in a deep-space laboratory on 7.2/4, 2488 C. The first crewed vessel, which launched in 2511 C, carried two passengers in a space smaller than a wardrobe. Scaling to useful cargo capacity required another decade of field geometry refinements. By 2520 C, reliable interstellar travel had become possible.

Priya Vishwanathan died in 2534 C, forty-six years after her breakthrough.
