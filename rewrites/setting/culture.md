# Culture and Society

## Languages and Communication

Linguistic Diversity: Linguistic fragmentation is common across human space. Planets with multiple, geographically isolated settlements often maintain dozens of unique local languages. Conversely, major powers frequently promote specific linguistic standards to unify their territories, such as the enforcement of Mandate Classical.

The Trade Tongue: To facilitate interstellar commerce and diplomacy, a lingua franca known simply as "Trade Tongue" has developed. While it is almost never spoken as a native language, it functions as a nearly ubiquitous second language along the great corridors—anyone who deals across star systems learns it, and anyone who doesn't deal across star systems mostly doesn't. Away from the corridors its writ thins: regional trade pidgins do its work in some volumes of space, rival linguae francae in others, and there are settled worlds where no one speaks a word of it. Even where it runs, eleven centuries of communication lag have pulled it into regional dialects that can take a newcomer a day's practice to follow.

Communication Lag: Real-time conversation between different star systems is impossible; the best you can do is a lag of several hours each way if there is a direct relay.

## Religion and Philosophy

No single religious settlement holds across human space, and any document that claims one is describing its compilers. The cosmopolitan corridors—the great hubs, the powers' core worlds, the places records like this one are written from—run largely secular, with diverse philosophical traditions doing the work that scripture does elsewhere, and it is easy to mistake that for the condition of the species. Step off the corridors and the picture changes world by world: entire regions held by old faiths carried out from Earth, syncretisms a thousand years deep with no name outside their own systems, and new religions founded every generation—a scattered humanity has never stopped producing gods. Some worlds are devout the way the corridors are secular: totally, and without noticing. The only safe generalisation is the lag—a faith, like everything else, travels at the speed of ships, which is why human space holds tens of thousands of them and no missionary has ever caught up.

## Social Organisation

Social and political structures vary enormously, with no single model of governance dominating human space. Notable examples include:
- Imperial hierarchies, such as The Mandate.
- Corporate governance, exemplified by the Sable Cartel.
- Feudal systems, such as the Vega Throne.
- Meritocratic republics, such as Arrhenos.
- Direct democracies, practiced by various independent worlds.
- Anarchic frontier communities, such as the Union Freeholds.
- Nomadic consensual governance, relied upon by the Drift Communities.
