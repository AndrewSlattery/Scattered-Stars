# The Shape of Human Space

There is no authoritative map of human space, for the same reason there is no galactic "now": every chart is a compilation of stale reports, and the farther from home it reaches, the older it is. What follows is the shape most compilers agree on, at the resolution where they still agree.

## The bubble

Human space is a lumpy, ragged region roughly a **thousand light-years across** at its widest, centred loosely—historically, sentimentally—on Sol. Within it lie the million-plus surveyed stellar bodies of the catalogues, of which some four thousand systems are inhabited: a thin scatter, not a filled volume. Between any two settled systems lie dozens that hold nothing but a survey beacon and a name, and most hold not even that. The bubble has no defended border and no agreed edge; it simply thins, system by system, into the unsurveyed dark, and the word "frontier" means wherever the thinning starts relative to whoever is speaking.

Direction, by convention, is galactic: **coreward** and **rimward**, **spinward** and **trailing**. The corridors and the powers are described in these terms, and so are the frontiers—of which there are as many as there are directions to thin out in.

## Where the powers sit

- **Sol** — and with it the **Neo-Solar Republic**—lies near the historic centre of the bubble, a dense, old, heavily-catalogued neighbourhood. **Vega** is Sol's near neighbour by interstellar standards, some twenty-five light-years out: the two oldest settled volumes in human space sit almost within hailing distance, which is to say a fifty-year round trip by radio.
- **The Mandate** sprawls coreward and trailing of centre—much the largest contiguous volume any power claims, hundreds of settled systems deep. Its throne world, **Tiamming**, lies roughly **180 light-years from Arrhenos**, the one interstellar distance every banking clerk in human space happens to know. The Mandate's trailing edge frays into contested space: the **Yansieve systems** sit out on that fraying edge, which is part of why the Bureau heard nothing until it was too late.
- **The Union of Frontier Freeholds** is not a volume but an arc—a broad, broken scatter along the bubble's trailing and rimward margins, wherever the settled thinning is thinnest. It adjoins the Mandate along a long, ill-defined border that creeps in the Mandate's favour by a system or two a decade. The **Sefkir Reach** sits far out on the trailing edge, remote even by Freehold standards, which is part of why its expulsion of the Cartel worked.
- **Arrhenos**, in the Mas system, sits in the well-travelled middle country between the Mandate's volume and the Sol–Vega neighbourhood—on nobody's border, athwart everybody's routes. The position is not luck. A neutral bank does well to be reachable from everywhere and surrounded by no one.
- **The Elysian Collective** has a moving capital and worksites wherever the contracts are; **the Drift** is wherever its havens presently are; **the Sable Cartel** is a set of ownership relations and has no location at all, which its analysts are tired of explaining.

## The corridors

The named trade routes—the Golden Circuit, the Power Highways, the Long Haul—are not roads but *habits*: chains of well-kept jump-shell infrastructure, dense catalogue coverage, and scheduled couriers, worn into the map by traffic the way a path is worn into grass. The **Golden Circuit** loops through the Sol–Vega neighbourhood, the Arrhenos middle country, and the Mandate's coreward face. The **Long Haul** spokes run rimward and trailing, out to the Freehold arc and beyond. Off the corridors, distance stops being measured in light-years and starts being measured in *waits*—for a courier going the right way, for a catalogue that mentions your destination, for anyone at all.

## The frontiers, plural

- **The trailing frontier** — the Freehold arc, the Sefkir Reach, the Mandate's contested edge, the Yansieve systems: the frontier of the news, where the map is currently being redrawn.
- **The Andromedan frontier** — the volume out beyond Vega space, toward the Andromeda constellation: scene of the Banking War's contested claims, still administered in part by Arrhene arbitration tribunals that will not close their dockets until 3890.
- **The rimward deeps** — thin, poor, and mostly quiet; the Long Haul's far ends.
- **The Empty Quarter** — a coreward-spinward volume that appears in the catalogues chiefly as a place routes do not go. See [Appendix E: Open Questions](../appendices/mysteries.md).

## The honest caveat

All of the above is corridor knowledge. A compiler on Tiamming and a compiler at Korsen's Anchorage would draw these regions with different centres, different edges, and different names for the same directions—and a compiler on a world three waits off the Long Haul might not recognise the map at all. Human space does not have a shape. It has as many shapes as it has vantages, and this document was written from the corridors, like nearly everything else you will ever read about the galaxy.
