# Haven Ascendant: Society

## Governance

The Captain-Archivist holds executive authority and scholarly leadership at once: elected for life by the Convocation and removable by supermajority, in command of the Haven's course and its external face, and the arbiter of its disputes. The office is barred from unilaterally altering the Core Codes or opening sealed archives.

The Convocation, of around 1,200 members, is the deliberative body: guild delegates at sixty per cent, residential delegates at thirty, and elder seats by seniority at ten. It debates policy, approves major spending, and elects or removes the Captain-Archivist. Sessions are public and frequently packed.

The seats are apportioned to guilds and residents, and the Outring's vast transient population—dockworkers on short contracts, crews between berths, the newly arrived—are mostly neither, and so are mostly unheard.

## The Core Codes

The foundational principles, amendable only by supermajority.

1. The Haven shall preserve.
2. The Haven shall remember: no deliberate forgetting.
3. The Haven shall move: remain jump-capable and independent.
4. The Haven shall welcome: entry refused only for threat, never for identity.
5. The Haven shall celebrate: joy is not frivolity but purpose. Added 2934 C, over scholarly objection.

Each has a price the Haven has chosen to pay. Independence means the constant cost of staying mobile and resupplied, and welcome has been defended, on hard occasions, with blood, because a sanctuary that turns nobody away for who they are will sometimes have to fight the people who came hunting them.

## Daily rhythm

A loosely kept twenty-hour cycle, synchronised to the C calendar and overruled in practice by the festival calendar, with major celebrations every few weeks and minor ones constant.

A representative day: breakfast in a Heritage Row room serving reconstructed twenty-third-century Lunar cuisine, a shift cataloguing in the Stacks, a Memory Theatre performance, dinner and drink with friends, a late hour at the Tournament Ground. For the Outring the same day has more shift in it and less theatre, but the festivals reach every deck and the Axis runs free.

## Language and dress

Trade Tongue is the Promenade default and the language of the wider Drift. Ascendant Drift, the native tongue, has evolved from it over centuries: thick with archival and preservation vocabulary, rich in words for memory and loss, and grammatically insistent on distinguishing the documented from the merely remembered. Most Haven-born are bilingual, and the Stacks keep specialists in over two hundred languages.

Dress runs to no uniform. Archivists favour the durable and muted, Promenade workers dress for their venues, and residents layer for the shifting temperatures and lean, given the choice, toward colour.

## Gap 2: Assessment and Feast

Every section reports its status—food, water, atmosphere, fuel, systems, population. It began in the desperate early decades as bare practicality and hardened into rite.

At midday the Captain-Archivist reads the Assessment summary from the Axis, broadcast through the whole station. Even the Promenade goes quiet. The message never changes: this is what we have, this is who we are, and we continue.

The evening scatters into thousands of simultaneous gatherings—every residential section, guild and Promenade district laying out its own food and drink, people moving between them along a free-running Axis until it all burns out somewhere past the next nominal dawn.

First we know what we have; then we celebrate it.
