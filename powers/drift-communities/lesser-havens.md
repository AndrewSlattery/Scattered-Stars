# The Lesser Havens: Two Portraits

[Haven Ascendant](haven-ascendant/index.md) is the Drift outsiders picture, and the overview is blunt about what it actually is: the luckiest deck in the fleet. This entry is the correction—two havens documented briefly, one from the working majority and one from the floor, offered the way Ascendant is offered: as examples, not templates. There are hundreds of thousands of others, and every one would quarrel with being represented by these.

---

## Haven Reliant

*A Trade Haven of the working majority.*

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Type | Trade Haven (jump-capable) |
| Population | ~85,000 |
| Age | ~400 years; rebuilt in sections, twice from serious damage |
| Circuit | An eleven-stop loop through the rimward Long Haul spokes, ~2 years per pass |
| Sells | Medicine, technical repair, neutral ground, news, a good week |

Reliant is a nine-hundred-metre rotating drum with four centuries of patches, and it is what most Drift life actually looks like: a town that flies. Its circuit serves eleven thin-settled systems that see no other regular visitor—worlds where "the haven is coming" is a calendar event, a market day, a clinic day, and the only reliable mail service, all at once. The surgery teams work double shifts the whole stop; the machine shops take in a season's broken equipment; the Long Gallery becomes a market and a dance floor by turns; and for a week or two the locals get the one thing a small world cannot make for itself, which is *elsewhere, visiting*.

The margins are thin and everyone aboard can recite them. Fuel and parts are bought at frontier prices; a slow season means deferred maintenance; deferred maintenance means the section meetings get quiet and arithmetical. Reliant has sold pieces of itself twice in living memory—a shuttle, then the big fabricator—and bought them back, once. Its accounts sit with [Nexus](../sable-cartel/fronts.md), because Nexus said yes and the [Arrhene houses](../../worlds/arrhenos/economy.md) that hold richer havens' reserves have no branch on the rimward spokes; the debt is modest, watched, and never quite gone. The [hard-won joy](overview.md) is not a brochure here. It is the Assessment kept honestly and the Feast thrown anyway, in a mess hall where everyone knows exactly what the ledger said that morning—which, the crew would tell you, is the only kind of feast that counts.

**Semma Uld** (51) has been Reliant's dockmistress for nineteen years and its de facto foreign minister forever: the face eleven worlds associate with the Drift entire. She keeps a ledger of favours owed and owing across the whole circuit—births attended, engines lent, a harvest saved in '54—that no auditor will ever see and no debt collector could ever price. *"The money books say we're poor,"* she says. *"The other book says eleven systems would fly out to pull us off a rock. Rich is having the second book."*

## The Quiet Haven

*A Refugee Haven; the floor of the fleet.*

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Type | Refugee Haven (jump-capable, barely) |
| Population | ~30,000, churning |
| Age | Unknown; the hull is old salvage, the community ~150 years |
| Name | Changes; outsiders' name for it has stuck better than any of its own |
| Sells | Nothing. Asks nothing. Takes people in |

It has had six names in a century and a half, and files no flight plan, and the Drift's network nodes pass word of its position the way one passes a house key: to the right people, quietly. The Quiet Haven takes in the people the galaxy is looking for or has thrown away—fled subjects with Bureau files, debtors two jumps ahead of a Nexus judgment, deserters, the disgraced, the simply erased—and its one law is the Drift's fourth Code kept at its hardest: entry refused only for threat, never for identity, and *no questions that outrun the offered answers*.

Life aboard is austere past anything Reliant knows: rationed water, salvaged everything, work assignments posted daily because the station survives on everyone's hands. It is not warm, exactly. It is *level*—a place where a Mandate registration band, a Vega debt notice, and an Aegis contract all mean the same nothing, and where the question "what were you?" is answered, if at all, at night, voluntarily, in the one commons where the lights are kept low on purpose. Some aboard heal and move on to the working fleet. Some stay and become the crew. A few are found even here, and what happens then is the reason the Haven's dock watch is the best-armed thing about it; the fourth Code has been paid for in blood aboard, more than once, and the crew reckons that the price of meaning it.

The richer havens fund it, discreetly—Ascendant among them, by a line in the Convocation budget labelled only *fleet obligations*—because the whole Drift understands what the Quiet Haven is: the floor under every deck, the proof that stateless does not have to mean unprotected, and the place any of them could end up, one bad decade from now. The Drift romanticises almost everything about itself. Not this. This it simply keeps flying, and does not photograph.
