# The Lesser Havens

Two havens documented briefly, one from the working majority and one from the floor. There are hundreds of thousands of others, and every one would quarrel with being represented by these.

## Haven Reliant

A jump-capable Trade Haven of around 85,000 people, some 400 years old and rebuilt in sections, twice from serious damage. Its circuit is an eleven-stop loop through the rimward Long Haul spokes, about two years to a pass, selling medicine, technical repair, neutral ground, news, and a good week.

Reliant is a nine-hundred-metre rotating drum with four centuries of patches: a town that flies. Its circuit serves eleven thin-settled systems that see no other regular visitor, worlds where "the haven is coming" is a calendar event, a market day, a clinic day and the only reliable mail service at once. The surgery teams work double shifts the whole stop, the machine shops take in a season's broken equipment, and the Long Gallery becomes a market and a dance floor by turns.

The margins are thin and everyone aboard can recite them. Fuel and parts are bought at frontier prices, a slow season means deferred maintenance, and deferred maintenance means the section meetings get quiet and arithmetical. Reliant has sold pieces of itself twice in living memory—a shuttle, then the big fabricator—and bought them back, once. Its accounts sit with Nexus, because Nexus said yes and the Arrhene houses that hold richer havens' reserves have no branch on the rimward spokes. The debt is modest, watched, and never quite gone.

Semma Uld, 51, has been Reliant's dockmistress for nineteen years and its de facto foreign minister forever: the face eleven worlds associate with the Drift entire. She keeps a ledger of favours owed and owing across the whole circuit—births attended, engines lent, a harvest saved in 3854—that no auditor will ever see. "The money books say we're poor. The other book says eleven systems would fly out to pull us off a rock. Rich is having the second book."

## The Quiet Haven

A Refugee Haven, jump-capable barely, holding around 30,000 people who churn. The hull is old salvage of unknown age; the community is about 150 years old. It has had six names in a century and a half, and outsiders' name for it has stuck better than any of its own. It sells nothing and asks nothing.

It files no flight plan, and the Drift's network nodes pass word of its position the way one passes a house key. It takes in the people the galaxy is looking for or has thrown away: fled subjects with Bureau files, debtors two jumps ahead of a Nexus judgment, deserters, the disgraced, the simply erased. Its one law is the fourth Code kept at its hardest—entry refused only for threat, never for identity—and no questions that outrun the offered answers.

Life aboard is austere past anything Reliant knows: rationed water, salvaged everything, work assignments posted daily because the station survives on everyone's hands. It is not warm, exactly. It is level: a place where a Mandate registration band, a Vega debt notice and an Aegis contract all mean the same nothing, and where the question of what you were is answered, if at all, at night, voluntarily, in the one commons where the lights are kept low on purpose.

Some aboard heal and move on to the working fleet. Some stay and become the crew. A few are found even here, which is why the dock watch is the best-armed thing about the station.

The richer havens fund it discreetly, Ascendant among them, by a line in the Convocation budget labelled only *fleet obligations*.
