# The Radiant Court

The royal palace complex on Lyra's Aurelian continent: some fifty square kilometres of buildings inside five hundred of grounds, holding around 200,000 staff, residents and permanent visitors.

## The complex

At its heart is the Central Palace—the Throne Hall for formal audiences, the Great Gallery, the Council Chamber, the royal apartments—old at its core and layered with centuries of additions, each expressing its era's taste while pretending continuity with the last.

Around it sit the administrative districts holding the Ministries, the Courts, the Archives and the Exchequers; the Noble Quarters, where visiting aristocrats are housed in accommodation graded precisely to rank, an archduke's townhouse a small palace and a baron's suite merely comfortable; and the Commercial Exchanges, where business meets government because proximity to the Throne is itself a tradeable asset.

## Precedence

Attendance at the great court occasions is not optional. Each rank owes a calibrated minimum of presence, and absence without an excuse accepted by the Chamberlain's office reads as disloyalty.

What governs every interaction is precedence—who stands where, enters when, speaks in what order—determined by rank, then seniority, then current commercial standing, then royal favour. The precedence lists are updated quarterly, so a house rising in the market is placed visibly ahead of an older house in decline, and the court reads the seating like a stock ticker.

| Maintenance         | ~30,000 | physical plant and grounds |
| Security            | ~20,000 | guard presence             |
| Ceremonial staff    | ~10,000 | event production, protocol |
| Household servants  | ~5,000  | direct royal service       |

Court service carries status—"served at the Court" opens doors elsewhere—and it is service. Petitioners who come seeking royal attention wait in designated areas, submit through proper channels, and may wait weeks or receive no answer at all.

## Reaching the Queen

Queen Lysandra IV is not constantly visible. She appears at the functions that require her and the occasions she chooses, and her absence is noted as sharply as her presence is prized. Reaching her means passing through layers, each with its gatekeeper: court presence through the Chamberlain's office, noble audience through the Lord Chamberlain, private audience through the Queen's Secretary, informal contact through the royal family itself.

Duke Vasilis Therenne has been Lord Chamberlain for nineteen years: meticulous, discreet, feared, loyal to the Throne rather than to any house, and careful to keep his own house's benefit from his position within tolerable bounds.

Lady Celeste Aurum, the Queen's Secretary, controls the royal schedule. She rose from modest minor nobility on civil-service competence and is loyal to Lysandra personally. What reaches the Queen's attention, Aurum decides.

Sir Marcus Varn, Captain of the Guard, and Countess Elara Silven, Mistress of the Robes, complete the inner ring, controlling the body and the image of the sovereign.

## Notable spaces

The Throne Hall is built to overwhelm—vaulted, vast, the throne raised so that supplicants must look up—and stages the receptions, ennoblings and major announcements. The Mirror Gallery runs four hundred metres of accumulated masterworks. The Star Gardens, planted with species that glow after dark, host the prestige evening functions. And the Commercial Floor of the Exchanges hums on through all of it: when the Ascension Ball turns in the Central Palace, the trades still clear next door.
