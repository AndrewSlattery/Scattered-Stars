# The Vega Commercial Throne: Media

Vega has no Bureau of Internal Harmony, no listeners and no disappearances. The Throne governs one and a half trillion people with content.

## Entertainment

Some 800 billion quid a year, employing around 40 million directly and perhaps 200 million indirectly across thousands of active productions, reaching most of human space.

The flagship format is the serialised drama, with narratives running for years or decades. *The Amber Throne* is in its sixty-seventh season, has outlived its original creators, and has put a phrase into Trade Tongue: to do an Amber Throne is to navigate a tangled situation with strategic patience. A prestige season costs fifty to a hundred million quid across forty episodes, and earns it back several times over in licensing and merchandise.

Around the serials orbit music built for live performance and relentless touring—the Core, the Mandate circuit, the Freehold run, the Drift stops—the growing trade in immersive formats, and the great spectacles like the Crown Tournament and the Artistic Olympiad, where noble houses sponsor competitors and bask in their wins.

## The talent pyramid

| Stars                 | hundreds              | excellent conditions; real negotiating power |
| Working professionals | tens of thousands     | steady and decent                            |
| Aspirants             | hundreds of thousands | poor; ferocious competition                  |
| Support staff         | millions              | variable; the invisible majority             |

The industry needs this shape, many candidates yielding few successes. Talent is signed, developed, used and dropped, and a face beloved this season is gone the next, replaced from an inexhaustible supply of the hopeful. The guilds—Performers', Writers', Technical, Musicians'—enforce minimum standards and arbitrate credit, and can do nothing about the economics. Houses tolerate them because a functional guild reduces friction, and would crush one that reached past that.

## News

Vega journalism is among the most trusted in human space, and the trust is earned. The reason is structural: the news houses are commercial rather than political, selling information to audiences who want it accurate, so competition rewards truth-telling, because a house caught lying loses the only thing it sells. House Meridian carries around forty per cent of Vega news consumption, keeps bureaux in every major system, and has four and a half centuries invested in its credibility.

The trust is also carefully bounded.

| Covered well      | external politics, business and commerce, foreign scandal |
| Handled carefully | the royal family, inter-house disputes, domestic noble scandal |
| Not seen at all   | common life, structural critique, labour conditions       |

The seam shows most at the Throne itself, where Prince Octavian runs Stellar Networks: professional, competent, and never quite willing to embarrass the family that owns it. Queen Lysandra is scrupulous never to be seen directing the news, tolerating Meridian's independence being cheaper than controlling it, and buying more.

## The foreign hand

Blackwell Media, a subsidiary of the Sable Cartel, has held a presence in the ecosystem since before the Banking War. Its outlets are modest and its content unremarkable. A media operation is a listening post, a channel and a lever, and the Cartel has never owned one for the advertising.

## Regulation

The Ministry of Culture regulates broadcasting and disburses patronage, keeping the apparatus aligned with the Throne without an order crude enough to notice. There is no censor's office worth the name.
