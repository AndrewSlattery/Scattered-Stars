# House Cassiline

Archducal, specialising in luxury fashion and textiles. Around 60,000 employees and some 35 billion quid a year. For centuries Cassiline simply was Vega fashion, having invented the aesthetic vocabulary the whole culture still calls Vega style.

## The wound that will not close

The Banking War struck Cassiline disproportionately: facilities damaged in contested systems, distribution severed, inventory lost, and—worst, because hardest to rebuild—its best designers gone, some to rivals offering stability and some of them to Valdorian. Luxury consumption collapsed in wartime, the post-war austerity prolonged the fall, and brand prestige, once visibly weakened, proved far harder to restore than any factory.

Twenty-three years on, Cassiline has recovered its production capacity and not its position. The haute division, Cassiline Couture, runs at half its former output: two seasonal collections where there were four, two thousand bespoke clients where there were five thousand, two hundred designers where there were five hundred. The premium line, Silken Thread, still earns the house's bread, and now does it against Valdorian Lifestyle, which entered premium fashion during the war with scale Cassiline cannot match.

## Duchess Isadora Cassiline

Sixty-two, presenting late thirties, head of the house since 3847 C. She inherited a struggling house from a father who died without resolving the war's aftermath, and has held the line by betting everything on traditional quality: rejecting the compromises that might raise volume, defending the brand against dilution, refusing Valdorian's repeated approaches, and waiting for excellence to be rewarded as she believes it must be.

Her critics call her rigid, in love with a past the market has left behind. Her supporters call brand identity the one asset worth protecting.

Her brother and heir, Lord Lucan Cassiline, who runs the house's fashion-content arm at Phoenix Studios, wants the opposite: volume, accessibility, branded content. The siblings' disagreement about whether to chase the market or outlast it is the house's real unresolved argument, conducted in private and known to the whole industry.

## The vice

Cassiline carries roughly 200 billion quid in syndicated reconstruction loans, at fifteen billion a year to service, with payoff projected for 3890 C if nothing goes wrong. The burden constrains everything that might reverse the decline: new capacity, talent, marketing, the acquisitions a stronger house would make. The house is solvent and unable to move.

Valdorian has approached about acquisition more than once and will approach again. Cassiline's prestige would complete Valdorian Lifestyle, and Cassiline's weakness is exactly the kind of opportunity Valdorian was built to take. Acquisition would mean the end of the family's independence and the absorption or quiet discarding of an identity centuries old. Isadora has refused each time.

## Holdings and relations

The house seats at Silken Reach on the Aurelian continent, arguably the most beautiful of the great estates and an expense the house can barely meet. It keeps the Cassiline Atelier on the Manufactories, where its couture is still made by hand.

Relations are hostile with Valdorian, cooperative with Therenne, since jewellery complements fashion, and quietly sympathetic with Aurelian, the other house declining from former heights. They are strained now with the royal house itself, which after generations of dressing exclusively in Cassiline has begun to wear Valdorian designs alongside it.
