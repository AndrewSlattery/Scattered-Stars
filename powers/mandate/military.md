# The Mandate: Military

The largest military in human space is built to deter other great powers, hold infrastructure, and win set-piece battles by mass. It fields some 50 billion active personnel against perhaps 200 billion reserves, with thousands of capital ships and tens of thousands of support vessels.

## Organisation

The Imperial Navy handles system defence, force projection, blockade and interdiction, and orbital bombardment, and is the senior service. Its officer corps is drawn disproportionately from citizen military families; a subject reaching flag rank is remarked upon.

The Imperial Army is larger and less glamorous—planetary defence, occupation, internal security and ceremony—and considerably more subject-heavy, because the military road to citizenship mostly runs through the ground forces.

The Imperial Guard combines the personal protection of the emperor and his family with special operations. Selection is brutal and service is for life.

Sector governors maintain their own forces for system defence, anti-piracy and emergency response. These are subordinate to Imperial command in principle and considerably autonomous in daily practice.

## Doctrine

Four principles are taught. Mass, on the reasoning that attrition favours the larger power. Control of infrastructure, since holding jump nodes and transit routes matters more than patrolling the space between them. Overwhelming response, so that defiance appears unsurvivable and is not begun. And patience, the Mandate being able to sustain an operation for longer than an opponent can sustain resistance.

In practice the forces are standardised, well equipped, heavily drilled, and rigid, with initiative discouraged below senior rank.

## Deployments

The Navy holds the lanes around the Yansieve systems and isolates the planets. The ground campaign that would retake them has not been authorised.

Forces are deployed to Kepler-442; purpose and status classified.

The L4 complex at High Tiamming remains the primary fleet base. It is quieter than it was three years ago, with ships, crews and matériel drawn off to the trailing frontier and those left behind working longer rotations.

## Service and citizenship

A subject who completes a fifteen- to twenty-year enlistment and is honourably discharged earns citizenship for himself, and by inheritance for his descendants. It is the commonest route across the line, and the Army's ranks are filled in no small part by men taking it. Parvati sends more of its subjects down this road than anywhere else in the Mandate. Veterans come home with the status they earned and a clear-eyed ambivalence about the system that made them earn it.
