# Xuan

The northern continent of Tiamming, some 8 billion people, holding the empire's political centre and its oldest lineages. It has supplied the administrative class for centuries and has concluded that nowhere else quite matters.

## Geography

Temperate, cooling to genuinely cold in the north—one of the few places on a climate-managed world where real winters still come, the management having been deliberately left thin there. Ancient forests, some grown from the original terraforming; mountain ranges; broad river valleys where the earliest settlements took root. The Songjiang runs from the northern highlands to the sea, with the Chrysanthemum Court at its mouth. Cities are strung along its course and agriculture fills the floodplains, while the highlands stay sparse.

## The estates

Beyond the capital, Xuan belongs to its aristocracy. Noble clans hold territories ranging from modest farms to regions the size of small countries. Mandate law applies uniformly and subjects retain their rights on paper, but the local lord shapes life in ways the distant capital cannot. These are not feudal holdings in the medieval sense; they are a second government, older than the modern Mandate, whose loyalty was bought long ago with autonomy.

The Vervain clan controls the Songjiang headwaters, and therefore the water of hundreds of downstream communities. Its wealth is ancient and its loyalty has gone unquestioned for seventeen generations. It is the clan the Throne calls on when it wants reliable support to look spontaneous.

The Ombros clan administers the Northern Reaches, where the climate management thins out and the winters are real. It breeds the cold-weather horses for the ceremonial cavalry and keeps a stern northern self-image to match—plain speech, physical hardiness, open disdain for southern softness. It has lately produced Marshal Cael Ombros, Minister of War and the loudest advocate of crushing the Yansieve rebellion.

The Kazan clan lost half its holdings when the previous generation involved itself in a coup. Mirek Kazan, now 84, has spent forty years buying the family's standing back with ostentatious loyalty and well-placed marriages, and has largely succeeded. His public statements run more orthodox than the emperor's, his donations to imperial causes are conspicuous, and his children have married into impeccably loyal families. He has been the loyal Lord Kazan for so long that the distinction between the role and the man may no longer exist, including for him.

For subjects the estates mean paternalism at best. A good lord provides schools, medicine, dispute resolution and a real if circumscribed security; a bad lord extracts labour, deference and whatever else he can reach. The law theoretically shields subjects from the worst, but the shield depends on someone reporting a violation, and reporting one's own lord—across the distance, the deference and the lord's own networks—is not a thing most people attempt twice.

## Regional culture

The Xuanese dialect is the prestige form of Classical, Xuanese cuisine sets court fashion, and Xuanese traditions are presented, by Xuanese, as simply the traditions.
