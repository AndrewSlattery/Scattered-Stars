# The Chrysanthemum Court

The Mandate's capital complex: four hundred square kilometres at the mouth of the Songjiang on the northern coast of Xuan, arranged in concentric rings of decreasing access. Metropolitan population around 500 million.

## The districts

The Forbidden Precincts are the walled compound at the heart, holding the throne room, the residential palaces, the most sensitive offices of state, and gardens laid out to represent the diversity of the empire. Entry is by imperial invitation, which most high officials never receive. 17 Xuanzong has ruled from inside these walls for forty-one years, through intermediaries, seen directly by very few.

The Ministries District, south of the Precincts, holds tens of thousands of civil servants in imposing stone halls: tax assessments, military logistics, infrastructure plans, subject registrations, citizenship applications, judicial appeals, diplomatic correspondence. The facades promise permanence; the interiors are cubicles and the low hum of procedure. It is also where the crush-contain-concede argument is actually conducted, in the form of disputes over budget lines, jurisdictional boundaries and the proper interpretation of precedent.

The Jade Quarters hold aristocratic compounds, ministerial residences, and wealthy citizens who want proximity to power. Clean streets, discreet security, impeccable service. Among the hereditary nobles and risen examination graduates lives the occasional wealthy subject who has bought a fine house and not yet bought citizenship.

The Thousand Markets are the commercial district, where Trade Tongue is heard as often as Classical, foreign merchants keep permanent establishments, and Drift shuttles dock to trade. The citizen-subject distinction blurs further here than anywhere else in the Court, though the registration bands are still there to be read.

The Old City predates the modern Mandate and preserves First Dynasty and colonial architecture, narrow streets, temples to discouraged faiths, festivals forgotten elsewhere, and dialects Classical was meant to bury.

The Sapphire Steps, where the High Courts convene, stand at the edge of the ministries.

## Life and security

For citizens the Court is comfortable: transit runs, services function, and the formality is a frame for a good life rather than a cage around it. Subjects work here rather than live here—staffing its service, construction and cleaning, commuting in from the surrounding districts and from Parvati. Visitors get the Thousand Markets, the Old City and the ministries' petition offices; the Jade Quarters and Forbidden Precincts are, for practical purposes, another planet.

This is the most secure location in the Mandate: Imperial Guard, Bureau surveillance, checkpoints at every district boundary, layered access controls. Most of it presents as orderliness rather than occupation.

Lady Fen Aravis keeps offices in the Ministries District and a residence in the Jade Quarters. Her presence at Court functions is noted by everyone and sought by no one.
