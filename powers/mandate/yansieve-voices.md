# Yansieve: Voices Through the Blockade

Nothing below can be verified. These are the things that come out of the blockaded systems by the routes such things travel, and they disagree with one another on nearly every point.

## From the log of a Freehold gun-runner

*The author ran the blockade at least four times for the arms trade. The log was sold at Veritas Station in 3861 C, once it was stale enough to be safe.*

Third run into Kessa Landing. What you hear in the Anchorage bars is that they're eating their boots in there, and what I can report is that the stevedores who offloaded us were thin and fast and paid on time, in kind, in rare-earth concentrate at a fair weight. Ribs on show, yes. Starving men don't work double shifts and they don't sing while they do it—and they sing, some anthem of theirs, new words to an old Mandate harvest tune.

The checkpoints are theirs now, and they run them exactly like the Bureau ran the old ones, wave-through for known faces and a cold hour for strangers. I said as much to my broker and she said, you build with the bricks you were raised in. No bands, though. First port I've ever worked where nobody's wrist says what they are.

## Broadcast fragment, the Free Systems Assembly

*Intercepted 3860 C. The speaker names herself only as a delegate for Merrow's World. Three versions circulate with three different endings; this is the shortest.*

…and they will tell you we are starving, and some seasons they are right. I will not stand here in front of the families of this district and pretend the winter was gentle. But I ask you to remember what we paid before, every year, in the quiet way that never made anyone's news—the sons who went into the registry queues and came out smaller, the wages that weighed less because our word weighed less. We have exchanged a tax we paid forever for a hunger that ends…

## Bureau of Internal Harmony, sector assessment

*Excerpt, 3861 C; classification lapsed in error.*

4.3. Factional divisions (negotiationist / separatist / the Merrow tendency) are genuine but are not, at present, load-bearing: external pressure remains the strongest unifying force acting on the rebel population, and the blockade is that pressure. The Directorate is invited to consider, as it has been invited before, that our principal instrument may be manufacturing the cohesion it is deployed to erode.

4.4. Food security in the inner systems is worse than rebel broadcasts admit and better than our own projections require. The projections have been revised four times. The Directorate is referred to the revision history rather than to the current figure.

4.7. Sampling of subject-population sentiment in [REDACTED] and Parvati remains within tolerances. The tolerances have been widened twice.

## Testimony of a refugee

*A water engineer of about fifty, formerly of Kessa, who paid a runner for passage out in the rebellion's second year. Deposited with the Chronicle Service at Haven Ascendant in 3861 C and unsealed at his own instruction, "because everything else from home is sealed one way or the other."*

People ask me which side I fled. Write this down exactly: I fled the war, not a side. On Kessa I repaired the same pumping station under three flags in two years—the Empire's, then the council's, then the council's again after the faction quarrel, which outsiders have never heard of and which cost forty lives in my town alone. The council men were my neighbours and they were brave and some of them were fools, and the second year they shot a hoarder who was not hoarding, and also they tore the registry office down with their hands and I cried like a child with everyone else. Both things. You want it simple and I cannot help you.

What I miss is the morning after the sweep ended. The first morning, before the blockade, before the shortages. Nobody working. Everybody in the street. A whole town standing in the road looking at each other, thinking: whatever we are now, we are not filed.

## Letter home from an Imperial Navy rating on blockade duty

*3862 C; passed by the censor, and shared afterwards by the family, which is how such things become documents.*

…mostly it is nothing, love, you must not imagine battles. It is watching a green world through instruments for ten hours and logging traffic that never comes. Sometimes a runner tries the shell and mostly we let the interdiction net do the work and mostly they turn back. Chao says we are the cork in a bottle the Emperor does not want to drink from or pour out, and Chao has been on report twice for saying cleverer things than that, so I only think it.

I will tell you the one thing, and the censor may keep it if he likes because it is only true: on the dark side of the third world you can see their cities. Lit. Four years, and the lights are still on down there. Nobody in this mess hall says anything about it, but everybody looks.
