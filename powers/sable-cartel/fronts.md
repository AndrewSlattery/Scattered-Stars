# The Sable Cartel: Known Subsidiaries

Five companies appear on nearly every analyst's list of probable Cartel holdings. Each is a real company that files real accounts, employs real people and does real, mostly legitimate business, around which a suspicion has hardened into near-consensus without ever quite becoming proof.

They are the ones large and visible enough to attract that suspicion and survive it, which makes them poor proxies for the Cartel's true shape. The assets that matter most are the small, dull holdings that move money and information without ever being interesting enough to investigate. The famous subsidiaries absorb the scrutiny.

The attributions below run from near-certain, in Wayline's case, to merely well-rumoured, in Blackwell's. At least one may not be Cartel at all.

## Nexus Financial Group

Capital Division. Founded 3341 C and acquired around 3500 C by estimate, headquartered at Korsen's Anchorage, some 400 million employees in banking, lending and financial services.

The Cartel's respectable face, and on the frontier a genuinely beloved one. Nexus lends where nobody else will—to stations and settlements and small polities the corridor houses would not return a call from—on fair terms, through patient officers who fly out to places the big banks have never seen. Ask a frontier harbourmaster about Nexus and you may get gratitude.

None of what follows a Nexus loan is visible from a teller's window. The four hundred million people who work for Nexus process loans, mind branches and go home. Nexus has been audited by every regulator with cause and every rival with a grudge, and the connection has never been proved.

## Wayline Logistics

Transit Division. Founded 2987 C and acquired around 3200 C by estimate, headquartered in distributed form with major hubs on Roughneck, Port Lophore and Tiamming Orbital, some 2 billion employees in interstellar shipping, warehousing and transit infrastructure.

The largest carrier in the Freeholds and a major one everywhere, and the surest attribution on this list. Two billion people draw a Wayline wage: dock crews, dispatchers, and long-haul spacers with their own dialects, superstitions and grievances renewed each contract. On a hundred frontier worlds Wayline is simply how things arrive, and its schedules serve as local clocks.

The smuggling that runs through it happens in the manifests rather than on the docks. A Wayline captain hauls what the paperwork says and is right to. Facilitation lives at the routing desks where a handful of managers decide which container is inspected lightly and which is not.

## Aegis Security Solutions

Security Division. Founded 3612 C, headquartered at Veritas Station, some 800 million employees in private military contracting, facility protection and executive security.

The respectable end of a disreputable trade. Aegis recruits heavily from Mandate veterans and Freehold militia and holds them to standards its competitors do not. Clients rate it highly, and its contractors are by and large professionals who believe they work for a professional firm, which they do. They learn otherwise only if they are unlucky enough to be standing on the wrong job the day it becomes a scandal.

## Verdant Holdings

Commodities Division. Consolidated from multiple acquisitions beginning in 3455 C, distributed headquarters, some 1.5 billion employees in agriculture, commodity trading and resource extraction.

A patchwork of farms, exchanges and extraction firms stitched together across a century of acquisitions, and the least conspiratorial-feeling company on this list, because most of what it does is grow food. Its company worlds carry three generations in the same valley towns, keeping harvest calendars and local shrines.

The strategic value is not the grain but the knowledge of the grain. Verdant's operations report continuously on what is grown and mined and shipped across hundreds of systems—a live map of supply that feeds the Intelligence Division and, not coincidentally, the fez desk's timing. Verdant rarely touches fez directly. It sells the harvesters their equipment and their provisions, and it watches, and it remembers.

## Blackwell Media

Intelligence Division. Founded 3721 C, headquartered at Stellavista in the Vega system, some 50 million employees in news, analysis and documentary production.

The thinnest attribution here, and the one the Cartel is happiest to neither confirm nor deny. By Vega standards Blackwell is nothing, a minor outfit dwarfed by the great media concerns, but its correspondents go where the big ones will not, into frontier zones and conflict regions, and its coverage is genuinely good.

A reporter embedded in a war is an intelligence asset whether or not she knows it, and Blackwell's people fall across the whole range from fully aware to entirely innocent. Whether Blackwell is truly Cartel-owned or merely Cartel-friendly is a question good analysts split on.

## Burned and unlisted

Two categories complete the picture and neither can be tabulated.

The first is the subsidiaries the Cartel has shed: companies exposed, disowned and cut loose, their connection severed at a mid-level desk and the rest left to fail alone. They do not appear on lists of Cartel holdings because by the time anyone looks closely they are no longer holdings.

The second, and far larger, is everything not named here—the holding companies, small banks, obscure exchanges and quiet logistics firms that do the real work precisely because nobody finds them worth the trouble. Analysts agree the true footprint is larger by some unknown multiple, and agree on little else.
