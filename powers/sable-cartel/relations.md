# The Sable Cartel: Relations

Every Great Power deals with the Cartel, and each one does it differently. The old line is that the Cartel cannot be excluded, its services too woven in to refuse. The neutral houses bank it at arm's length and prosper; the Sefkir Reach threw it out and survived being poorer for it; Cold Harbour, which honours no outside paper at all, has never had a Cartel problem to speak of.

## The Mandate

The Cartel's largest market and the source of its most determined enemy.

Mandate citizens and subjects use Cartel shipping, credit and insurance daily, and the Mandate's own government is among its better customers: Wayline carries military freight under contract, and Nexus lends to frontier governors the central treasury would rather not be seen funding.

The Bureau of Internal Harmony has worked for decades to map the Cartel inside Mandate space, and for decades has run aground on the Veil, naming subsidiary after subsidiary and never climbing above them. There are Bureau analysts who have given whole careers to hunting a single hand behind a thousand local hands, reading every dead end as proof of the hand's brilliance rather than its absence.

The flash point is Yansieve. If Cartel subsidiaries are running the blockade, and some surely are, then a commercial nuisance becomes a wartime one.

## Union of Frontier Freeholds

The Freeholds are built for the Cartel: minimal regulation, fragmented authority, and an ideology that recoils from telling anyone what they may not do. Subsidiaries operate in the open across most of Freehold space, and a good many Freeholds court them outright, because Cartel investment buys roads and ports and clinics nobody else is offering.

Others have watched the pattern run to its end and pushed back. The cautionary tale and the inspiring one are the same event: in 3829 C the Sefkir Reach expelled every Cartel subsidiary it could find and did not collapse. Freehold debating halls have not stopped arguing over whether that was a tragedy or a price worth paying.

The flash point is Korsen's Anchorage, where Nexus is headquartered and the Cartel's Freehold presence is least deniable.

## Vega Commercial Throne

When the Banking War broke the Vega houses, Cartel-affiliated lenders arrived with reconstruction capital on terms that looked merciful and were, above all, patient. Twenty-three years on, several houses owe money to creditors they cannot prove are Cartel and cannot comfortably repay, and find that certain decisions come easier when they happen to suit a lender's preference. The Capital Division never presses.

Beneath the ledger runs a cultural dislike the debt only sharpens. The Vega aristocracy is bred to inherited rank; the Cartel is the galaxy's purest meritocracy, indifferent to blood and quietly amused by titles.

## Neo-Solar Republic

The Republic has what the Cartel wants—Lattice neural technology, advanced computational substrates, physics it cannot buy legally—and guards it as a matter of survival.

What makes it a hard target is not its defences but its medium. Cartel tradecraft runs on opacity, on people who give away nothing they are not paid to give away; inside the Lattice, deception is costly and exposure is quick. An operative who is not integrated stands out; one who is risks being read.

The Republic watches Cartel-flagged vessels in Sol space through the Synthesis Defence Collective and assumes, correctly, that the Cartel is the likeliest author of any attempt on its research. Relations are minimal and cold. This is the rare relationship in which the Cartel is the less comfortable party.

## Elysian Collective

The two meet mainly in terraforming logistics, where Cartel subsidiaries bid to supply Collective projects and the Collective, jealous of its methods, awards warily. The disputes are real and small. Neither tries to subvert the other, and the upshot is the calmest relationship the Cartel keeps with any power, precisely because neither expects loyalty of the other, only consistency.

## Drift Communities

The Drift hold no fixed infrastructure and keep no fixed schedule, which makes them poor customers for most carriers and fair ones for Wayline, which will service routes others will not. Cartel logistics and Drift convoys meet at the trade hubs—Korsen's Anchorage above all, where a Drift waystation and the Cartel's most visible bank share a concourse.

The wariness is mutual and mild. The Drift, whose trust lives in memory and in reputation passed mouth to mouth, are constitutionally suspicious of an organisation that keeps everything in ledgers and shows nobody its face. The Cartel finds little here worth taking, nomadic populations being thin markets and poorer leverage.

## The neutral houses

A dozen or so houses across human space make their living keeping money that must survive its own politics, and the Cartel banks with most of them: the Reyes Trust out rimward, the Khivan trade families on the coreward Circuit, the Arrhene Consortium in the Mas system, and a scattering of smaller firms in their own regions.

Each takes the business on tighter terms than it imposes on anyone else—accounts monitored, transactions flagged, dedicated teams picking over Cartel-associated flows as a standing cost of the relationship. The Cartel submits, because neutral clearing is a thing it can neither reproduce nor do without, and because a house that will not take the business is replaced by one that will. The houses take it because excluding the Cartel would cost more than watching it does, and because watching pays: the intelligence gleaned from its transactions is a quiet export, sold on selectively.

This is not a leash. No house holds enough of the Cartel's business to threaten it, and the Cartel could lose any one of them in a season. What it could not survive is losing them all at once, and the reason it never has is that they have never once managed to agree on anything—which is a problem the Cartel recognises, being built the same way.

The trade itself is the Cartel's mirror. The neutral houses arbitrage the slowness of information in the open and call it banking; the Cartel does the same in the shadows and calls it nothing at all.
