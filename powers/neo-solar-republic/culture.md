# Neo-Solar Republic: Culture

## The ordinary Republic

Most citizens sit between EX 1 and EX 3: enhanced, not transformed. Material need is not a daily concern, and two healthy centuries is the ordinary expectation, spent as people with time spend it—on craft and study and family and long elaborate hobbies.

They work, though not always because they must. The socionomy meets needs without anyone's labour, so work is increasingly something one does for meaning, status, or the plain pleasure of being useful. Someone keeps the reactors running and the relay nodes synchronised and the morphological clinics staffed; someone teaches the children, and there are still children; someone cooks, for the considerable minority who still eat.

## The vanguard

At the Republic's leading edge are the citizens who take its possibilities literally. They wear bodies built for vacuum or deep ocean or no environment at all, run as distributed intelligences across a dozen substrates at once, merge for a season's work and part again, and fork themselves to live two lives at the same time. They make the Republic's reputation and most of its art. The ordinary majority regards them with a mix of pride, bafflement and faint wariness.

## Going dark

Enhancement is genuinely optional, and a real minority of citizens live lightly integrated—basic implants, a single body, the Lattice kept at arm's length—or refuse the Lattice altogether and go, in the local idiom, dark. Some are religious or philosophical objectors, some immigrants from low-enhancement worlds, and some simply never took to it.

The Republic does not persecute them. It leaves them out. Political voice runs through the Lattice, and a citizen who is not on it has no resonance, no vote, and no share in the collective mind that decides everything.

## The question of the self

When you can change your body, copy your mind, merge with another and persist without flesh, the question of who you are becomes a matter of law.

Continuists hold that the self survives any change so long as subjective continuity holds, memory and narrative making the person. Processualists hold that there is no persistent self at all, only patterns that feel continuous, and find this liberating rather than bleak. Collectivists hold that the individual is a legacy unit, and that the real person is the network of minds that think together.

Which camp prevails decides who inherits, who is liable, who is married to whom, and who is even one person.

## What it costs

Forks who were one person and have grown into two who cannot stand each other, dividing a life and a family between them. Merges that took badly, leaving a mind that is neither of the two it was made from. Digital citizens who have run so long, and drifted so far, that their oldest friends no longer recognise the pattern. Families spread across bodies and substrates and forks, working to stay families across differences no counsellor was ever trained for.

The Republic meets these with support, counsel and a whole vocabulary of care, and some of it cannot be fixed, only grieved.

The starkest record is the Registry of Endings: the roll of citizens who have truly and permanently ceased—not the body-death the Lattice makes survivable, but the deliberate deletion of a mind that could have gone on. People end themselves in the Republic too.

## The Census

Every ten years the Republic holds the Centennial Census, a binding decision about what counts as a person.

| 3840 C | purely digital citizens  |
| 3850 C | forked individuals       |
| 3860 C | collective intelligences |

Each was controversial inside the Republic and alarming outside it. A vote that can enlarge personhood can also withhold it, and every expansion has left citizens on the losing side. The Collectivists' logic, followed all the way, ends with the individual dissolved into the network, and each Census takes a step that way.

## Art

Republic art is experienced rather than observed. Its dominant form is the synaesthetic composition: a made experience transmitted through the Lattice, weaving image, sensation, emotion, abstract structure and narrative into a single unbroken thing that lasts seconds or hours. The finest are held to be untranslatable.

Beyond them lie shared dream-states, collaborative spaces that many citizens build and inhabit together, some sustained for decades by rotating hands. Against all of it runs a counterculture of physical art—paint, stone, instruments played with actual fingers—prized precisely because it refuses the Lattice.

## Language and customs

Among themselves, citizens rarely use words, the Lattice carrying concept and reasoning and felt experience without the lossy squeeze of language. Citizens call ordinary speech talking through a keyhole. It is not a universal solvent—different cognitive architectures parse thought differently, and misunderstanding survives even here—but it makes Republic gatherings unnerving to outsiders, who see a room sit silent and motionless while a fierce argument runs through it.

Citizens greet one another with a brief Lattice packet of identity, mood and intent, exchanged faster than a nod. Visitors are fitted with a limited interface for translation and navigation, never cognitive integration, and assigned a Liaison to guide them.

A citizen whose body dies but whose mind persists is not dead. A citizen who deletes a living mind is, body or no.
