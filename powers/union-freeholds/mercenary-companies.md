# Mercenary Companies of the Frontier

The Freeholds export violence, and it is one of their most reliable industries. Some fifty major companies and five hundred minor ones, plus thousands of independent operators, field around 8 million active personnel for something near 200 billion quid a year. Mercenary work is legal throughout the Freeholds and regulated, if at all, only by individual Freehold law.

## The industry

The frontier breeds mercenaries the way it breeds prospectors: the same risk tolerance, the same practical competence, the same absence of any authority to forbid it. The buyers are Freeholds wanting defence, anti-piracy and crisis response; corporations wanting asset protection, facility security and strike-breaking; other polities wanting deniable operations; and private individuals wanting security, debt collection or revenge. The richest contracts come from outside—the Mandate, Vega houses, and above all the Cartel—for capabilities they cannot or will not build in-house, and for distance from what they buy.

Companies sort roughly by how much their reputation is worth to them: elite firms, professional and selective and reputationally careful; standard contractors, competent and profit-driven and ethically variable; low-end operators of marginal quality; and outright criminal fronts, piracy and extortion wearing a mercenary label.

## The major companies

Iron Vanguard is the largest and most prestigious: founded 3412 C, some 180,000 active, headquartered at Veritas Station, built on combined-arms professionalism and the selling point of refusing certain work—no operations against civilians, no clients who fail due diligence. Its director, Kaela Sarn, sets the industry's tone. Its current contracts include a significant Yansieve presence, on which side it does not say.

Void Reapers (3567 C, ~95,000, Roughneck orbital) are the aggressive end of respectable: ship seizure, boarding, hostile recovery. Effective and ruthless, trailing wrongful-death settlements and a standing ban from two of the corridor insurers, and rumoured Cartel connections they neither confirm nor trouble to deny.

Stalwart Shield (3689 C, ~60,000, Korsen's Anchorage) are the defensive specialists—facility security, route protection, evacuation—reliable, conservative and proud to be boring. Insurers require their contracts and banks accept their protection as collateral.

Crimson Lance (3501 C, ~25,000, headquarters classified) does the work clients prefer not to discuss: reconnaissance, sabotage, targeted elimination, sensitive acquisitions. Nobody outside the company knows where it is based, recruitment is by invitation, and contracts come through intermediaries. Rumours of Mandate intelligence ties, Cartel operations and political assassination are met with the silence that is the firm's entire brand.

Freeborn Brigade (3623 C, ~40,000, Redoubt) refuses the Mandate and anyone aligned with it, trains resistance movements, defends populations under Imperial threat, and is rumoured to work at reduced rates or for nothing where the cause is righteous enough. Its involvement in supporting the Yansieve rebellion is among the worst-kept secrets on the frontier.

## Veritas and the Compact

The industry's centre is Veritas Station, where the major companies keep permanent quarters, contracts are negotiated face to face, and the Veritas Compact sets the informal rules: neutrality between signatories, contract registration and arbitration, shared intelligence on non-payers, and a ban on poaching active personnel. The Compact's arbitration works because everyone benefits from its working, and a company that defies a ruling finds future business hard to come by.

The Cartel's security subsidiary, Aegis, is also headquartered at Veritas, among the independents and indistinguishable at a glance from any other firm renting quarters and registering contracts.

## Recruitment and equipment

Companies recruit from former militia, Mandate deserters, frontier natives chasing opportunity, and the mercenary families in which the trade runs. The elite firms select rigorously; the low-end take anyone. They equip from Freehold manufacturers, external suppliers, captured stock and grey markets, the best maintaining standardised kit and the rest using whatever comes to hand. There is no Union-wide regulation, so the industry self-polices through reputation and through the Contract Registry at Veritas, which records agreements voluntarily and makes a firm's history its collateral.
