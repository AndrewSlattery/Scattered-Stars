# Roughneck

The industrial heart of the frontier: a functioning industrial economy three centuries past its rush, with an owning class, a working class, and a settled inequality. Around 890 million people planetside and 150 million in orbit, at 1.12 gravity, around Hephaestus Prime.

## From strike to settlement

Hephaestus was surveyed in 3456 C and dismissed as uninteresting until the prospector Callum Vance found, in 3512 C, that its crust held vast deposits of rare earths, heavy metals and industrial ore—hard to reach, but enormous. The rush was textbook. What made Roughneck rare is that its boom never busted: the deposits were deep enough to sustain centuries of extraction, deep mining created permanent work, and processing, then manufacturing, then services followed, until by 3600 C Roughneck was a diversified industrial economy.

With stability came concentration. Mining wealth gathered among claim-holders, facility owners and investors while workers watched their boom wages erode as the labour supply caught up. The Labour Wars of 3672–3689 C followed—strikes, lockouts and intermittent violence—and ended not in victory but in compromise: worker representation in the major facilities, minimum safety standards, some profit-sharing, and no change at all to who owned what. Labour relations have been tense and functional ever since.

## A class society

| Owners           | claim-holders, major shareholders, facility proprietors |
| Management       | administrators, engineers, specialists                  |
| Skilled workers  | miners, machinists, technicians                         |
| Unskilled labour | general labour and service positions                    |

Mobility exists and is limited. A skilled worker may reach management; management rarely reaches ownership; the mining fortunes have compounded down the generations into something very like an aristocracy that the frontier would be embarrassed to call one. Workers organise through skill guilds, general unions and the company councils the Labour Wars won them.

Governance is a frank hybrid: an elected council for civil matters, an industrial board of the major facilities for economic policy, and joint committees for the overlap. The arrangement hands the owners disproportionate weight on the board and has resisted every worker attempt to change it.

## Geography and cities

A heavy, rugged world—thick air, hard terrain, survivable without suits in most places and pleasant in none. The Scar is the primary extraction zone, the Basin feeds the planet, the Reaches are new development, and the Wastes are the depleted ground abandoned operations leave behind. The Roughneck orbital handles processing, shipyards and trade, and runs much of its bulk transit through Cartel Wayline logistics.

## Figures

Governor Bryn Thane, 58, elected head of the planetary council, is the career moderate both sides distrust, which she takes as proof she is doing it right. "This world works because everyone gets something. Owners get profits, workers get wages, the council gets stability. My job is making sure nobody tries to take everything."

Jace Holloway Sr, 64, has organised Roughneck's workers for forty years and thinks the old settlements have rotted. "The owners need us more than we need them. They've forgotten that." He is planning something larger—coordination across facilities, perhaps a general strike—pending negotiations now under way.

Director Darrow Vance, 71, descendant of the original prospector, controls more of Roughneck's extraction than anyone. "My family built this world. We've earned what we have. The workers earn what they're paid, nothing more." He is old, hardening, and pressed from two sides: by the workers below and by younger owners who think his refusal to bend is what will break the peace.
