# The Sefkir Reach

A cluster of a dozen or so Freeholds out toward the trailing edge, perhaps three billion people, remote even by frontier standards. In 3829 C the Reach expelled every identifiable Sable Cartel subsidiary from its systems and has never let them back.

## The expulsion

Through the 3820s the Cartel's subsidiaries had done in the Sefkir systems what they do everywhere: Nexus credit funding the booms, Wayline freight running the only reliable supply line, cheap subsidiary goods undercutting the local trades until the local trades were gone. What made Sefkir different was arithmetic and distance. It was remote enough that the Cartel's logistics were thin and expensive anyway, and small enough that a debt squeeze in 3828 C fell on everyone at once and visibly.

A dozen Freeholds that had never agreed on anything signed the Sefkir Compact in 3829 C: a mutual undertaking to expel every Cartel subsidiary from every member's systems on the same day, to refuse Cartel credit and Cartel freight thereafter, and—the clause that gave it teeth—to treat any member that quietly let the Cartel back in as having left the Compact.

It held. The subsidiaries were given thirty days, the docks that had been Wayline's became the Compact's, and the debts owed to Nexus were repudiated, which the credit market quietly noted and the Reach quietly accepted as the cost of the divorce.

## The price

Cut off from Wayline, the Reach built its own freight: a logistics cooperative of mismatched hulls that runs slower, breaks more, and reaches fewer places than the Cartel routes it replaced. Cut off from Nexus credit, it learned to live within its means and finances what it can through the Reyes Trust, having expelled the Cartel rather than all of finance—a distinction it is careful to keep. Cut off from the cheap subsidiary goods, it makes its own, badly and dearly, or does without.

Prices in the Reach are higher, choices fewer, the booms smaller and the busts harder. Some of the dozen founding Freeholds could not stand it and drifted back into the wider frontier's orbit within a decade. The committed core stayed.

What it bought is the absence of the chains the rest of the frontier wears: no company stores, no scrip good in only one place, no debt that cannot be traced because there is no Cartel to trace it to, no neutral commons that turns out to be someone's headquarters.

## The Cartel's patience

The Cartel did not crush the Reach. A Reach burned to the waterline would be a martyr; a Reach left visibly poor is a warning. So the Cartel manages the story—ensuring the hardship is seen and the freedom is not, letting the slow freight and the dear goods speak for themselves, and discouraging, by means that never quite surface, the brokers who might carry the Reach's example outward dressed as something enviable rather than something pitiable.

A handful of neighbouring Freeholds study the Reach with an interest that has lately turned from amusement to something more thoughtful.

## Figures

Edda Voss (d. 3851 C) is the Reach's nearest thing to a founder: a salvage captain turned organiser who, in the squeeze of 3828–29, did the unglamorous work of getting a dozen proud and suspicious Freeholds into one room and keeping them there until they signed. She refused every honorific the Reach tried to give her—"we did it; I just booked the room"—governed nothing afterward, and died poor and at home.

Sera Voll, 24, is Reach-born, Reach-raised, and old enough now to leave. The wider frontier is a few weeks' slow freight away, with its booms and its bright goods and its credit for anyone willing to wear the collar, and a young woman with Voll's hands could do well out there. She has not decided. "My grandmother signed the Compact. My mother's never once regretted it. And I've never once eaten as well as the people who took the deal."
