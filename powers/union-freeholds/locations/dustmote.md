# Dustmote

A Freehold in good standing whose natives cannot leave it. Not will not—cannot. Three centuries ago their founders rewrote their own genetics to require the specific light of Karenna, an F5V white star, and the change breeds true. Around 340 million people at 0.94 gravity, on a gentle agricultural world.

## The Rootbound Covenant

Karenna was surveyed in 3156 C and found mild in climate, stable in star and fertile in soil. The consortium that won the colony, the Rootbound Covenant, held that humanity's sickness was rootlessness and that the cure was to make leaving impossible. Before landfall they modified themselves—skin chemistry tuned to Karenna's spectrum, a dependency on trace compounds produced only under that light, developmental patterns requiring exposure in childhood—and made the changes heritable.

It was entirely legal, the Freeholds imposing no limit on self-modification, and it was widely condemned then and since as binding the unconsenting. The Covenant's answer was that all children everywhere are bound by their parents' choices, and at least these would have roots. The natives hold every position in the argument: some defend the founding as necessary, some resent it, most simply live inside it.

## The dependency

A Dustmote native deprived of Karenna's light declines within days, sickens within weeks and dies within months. The spectrum cannot be perfectly reproduced; equipment approximates it well enough for short trips and worse the longer one is away, so off-world life is possible, difficult, and a slow medical emergency. The modifications show—skin faintly orange, eyes golden in certain light, a sensitivity to anything outside the optimal range—so off-world a Dustmoter is recognisable, and meets the usual human menu of curiosity, prejudice and fetish.

## A society built for forever

The population has nowhere else to be and has built for permanence: durable infrastructure, multi-generational institutions, and one of the more sophisticated governments on the frontier, with an elected assembly by proportional representation, an executive council, an independent judiciary and real constitutional protections.

Its values follow from its condition. Commitment is the cardinal virtue. Stewardship holds that every generation owes the next an enduring colony. Self-sufficiency follows from the observation that to depend on imports is to depend on people who can leave.

Karenna's light and soil make Dustmote a serious agricultural exporter. It imports most complex technology while exporting a small sophisticated line of light-spectrum and life-support equipment, and consumes external media avidly, participating in the frontier mostly at a distance. Most natives never travel, and the few who range widely become a small worldly class.

At Roothold's centre stands the Shrine of First Light: a preserved habitat module from the colony ship, the founders' recorded explanation of their choice, and the genetic record of the modification. It is neither celebrated nor condemned, simply kept.

## Figures

Councillor Tashen Mor, 61, is Dustmote's representative to the Union and one of the few who travels widely, living by constant medical management of her dependency. "I've seen the frontier—the transience, the rootlessness. My ancestors wanted something different. They achieved it. Whether the cost was justified, ask me again on my deathbed."

Dr Yeren Cole, 45, a geneticist at Farlight, researches the reversal of light-dependency—work some hail as liberation and others damn as betrayal of the founders' vision. "Staying should be a choice, not a sentence." The work is decades from application, if it is possible at all.

Kenna Bright, 38, captains the small trader *Lasting Light*, whose quarters are rigged with Karenna-spectrum equipment so she can spend months away and pay only in slow medical cost. "Someone has to connect us to out there. The isolation would kill us slower than leaving kills me."
