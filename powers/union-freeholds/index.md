# Union of Frontier Freeholds

A confederation of some four thousand recognised Freeholds and around 1.7 trillion people, founded on the principle that nobody should be able to make you do anything. It has no capital and no standing authority. The Charter forbids any body to tax, conscript, regulate or command a Freehold against its will, and the Chair rotates yearly by lottery.

For a great many of its people the Freeholds are the only place in human space they could be who they are: the prospector who answers to nobody, the refugee the Mandate cannot reach here, the commune or corporation or one-ship family free to govern itself by whatever rule it can agree on. Freedom here is also privately owned, and held as unevenly as the wealth and the guns. A worker paid in company scrip he can spend only at the company store is free to leave in the sense that no law stops him and no ship will carry him, and with no authority above the single Freehold there is nobody to appeal to when the master is your employer, your creditor, or the corporation that holds your station's only dock.

## The Charter

The Freeholds emerged from the Cascade of 2756 C. Where most survivors of that year built new states, the Freeholds' founders drew the opposite lesson, and at the Korsen Conference of 2761 C rejected fourteen proposed constitutions before adopting the Charter of Freeholds—a document remarkable chiefly for what it refuses to contain. No central government, no binding authority, no power to tax, conscript or regulate. One substantive rule: no Freehold may claim sovereignty over an unwilling population. And a Chair whose powers have been cut twice in eleven centuries and were never large.

The one rule they wrote is the one rule nobody is empowered to enforce.

## What a Freehold is

Any settlement that declares itself one, is recognised by three others, governs itself, and claims no unwilling subjects. They range from a single mining station to a system of billions, and govern by every means humans have devised—direct democracy, corporate board, commune, proprietorship, a captain's word. The Union recognises about four thousand at any time, and the number shifts constantly as settlements rise, merge, splinter and bust. Each holds one vote regardless of size.

## The capture

The Mandate proved in the Grey Incursion of 3567 C that the Freeholds cannot be conquered: it occupied three systems and discovered there was nobody to make the rest surrender. What cannot be conquered can be bought.

The frontier's two great neutral institutions belong to the Sable Cartel. The indispensable bank at Korsen's Anchorage is the Cartel subsidiary Nexus, and the mercenary clearing-house at Veritas Station houses the Cartel's security arm, Aegis. The debt that funds every boom is outside debt—the Khivan trade families and the Reyes Trust at the reputable end, money of every flag beneath them—and a defaulting Freehold finds its options narrowing without anyone raising a hand. Meanwhile the Mandate's border creeps in system by system, and the shared beacons and relays decay, because a commons nobody is obliged to fund is a commons slowly abandoned.

In 3829 C the Sefkir Reach expelled every identifiable Cartel subsidiary and survived, poorer and slower and entirely its own. It paid for that alone, there being no Union to pay for it.

| Korsen's Anchorage | The frontier's marketplace and neutral ground; the Cartel's Nexus seat            |
| Veritas Station    | Mercenary and intelligence hub; home of the Veritas Compact, and of Aegis         |
| Dustmote           | A Freehold whose people remade themselves unable to leave                          |
| Roughneck          | A boom that grew up into a working industrial world, complete with an owning class |

## Notable relations with other Great Powers

| Sable Cartel      | Owns the frontier's commons and disciplines by debt what no fleet could hold |
| The Mandate       | Ideological enemy and creeping neighbour. Fleeing subjects find a door here that the Bureau cannot close, and Freehold guns quietly run the Yansieve blockade |
| Drift Communities | Natural kin—both stateless, and meeting at Korsen's                          |
| The Khiva Ascendancy | A nine-system regional power on the coreward Circuit whose trade families finance frontier ventures the great powers will not look at, and whose envoys are the only ones who arrive expecting to be taken seriously |
