# The Garden

The Collective's mobile headquarters: a deep-space station some 600 years old and continuously expanded, holding around 3 million permanent residents. It is the guild's centre of operations, its only school, its memory and its showcase.

The Collective holds no territory beyond it—only this moving capital and a scatter of worksites. Thirty to fifty worlds are under terraforming at any time, each a long-lived camp; the Lazarus sites are the hardest and deadliest postings; and dedicated research stations handle the advanced work. Most of the guild's people are not at the famous address but out on the projects, in prefab camps under hostile skies, for years or decades at a stretch.

## Where it is

The Garden keeps no fixed orbit. It moves between systems on a schedule set by the Council—near active projects, near resupply, or simply into deep space far from anyone—and its position is known only to the Council, the senior navigation staff and the pilot cadre. Invited guests are carried in aboard guild ships from designated meeting points and never learn the route.

It moves under conventional drives, with periodic gate transit when distance demands. Pushing a station of the Garden's mass through a jump gate is enormously expensive, and the Collective pays it without complaint.

## The biosphere

A vast interior biodome under an artificial sky, holding living specimens from every world the Collective has made. Temperate forest runs up against tropical wetland, alpine meadow sits above desert, and water threads through all of it. Visitors used to the thin biospheres of ordinary stations describe the experience as overwhelming, the air thick with scent and birdsong.

It does not run itself. A standing body of biologists and keepers manages the species balance, watches for collapse, and folds in new specimens as projects finish, much of the daily labour done by Tenders and civilian staff. Six centuries, members will tell you, without a single biosphere failure.

## The sections

The Conservatory holds the formal collection: catalogued specimens from every Collective world, kept in controlled environments that reproduce their home conditions. It is part museum, part gene bank and part insurance—if a terraformed world's ecosystem fails, the Conservatory holds the material to rebuild it—and ranks among the most complete biological archives in human space.

The Academy is the guild's only school and the gate every sworn terraformer passes through. Apprentices spend five to ten years here in theory, technique and guild culture, learning across disciplines before they specialise, on the principle that an atmospheric engineer who cannot read a biosphere is no use to anyone. Instructors are senior Masters rotating through between projects, who mostly regard teaching as a duty and return to the field as soon as they decently can. It is also where the Collective's class line is drawn each year.

The Archive holds records of every terraforming project in human space, Collective or otherwise: reports, environmental data, Genesis specifications, the world-songs, and the documentation of failures that feeds the Lazarus Project. Access requires Council authorisation, and even senior Masters cannot wander it freely.

The Council Chamber is a circular space at the station's core where the Council sits and, once a year, the full Conclave convenes. It is built for the unaided voice—no amplification, by a tradition older than the station's current form—and deliberately bare, with no ornament and no display of rank.

The residential districts fold the station's engineering into its greenery, with homes opening onto green space, paths winding through planting, and simulated light and biosphere-filtered air that make the place feel like ground rather than orbit. A berth on the Garden is the guild's highest material reward, extended to members, their families, and the civilian staff the city cannot do without. The waiting list is long: many of the sworn spend their best decades on project sites before a berth comes free, and many Tenders are promised the Garden their whole lives and die having seen it only in the world-songs.

## Defence

The Garden carries long-range sensors for early warning, a small escort fleet of armed ships, and point defences against anything that reaches it. Its surest protection is that a station nobody can find is a station nobody can hit. After that comes neutrality, which the Council is not so naive as to trust on its own.
