# Premise

**Working title:** *The Glitch in the Hesperine Plateau* — the [Hesperine Plateau](../../worlds/arrhenos/locations/brovdingonai/index.md) being the upland Brovdingonai sits on, and *the glitch* being Milo himself: a man who does not belong in this century, running an operating system the world was never written for.

## The pitch

A lonely man from Earth, 2025, is thrown roughly eighteen hundred years and an unguessable distance into the future, and wakes in a back-alley of [the Shambles](../../worlds/arrhenos/locations/brovdingonai/shambles.md) — the chaotic market heart of a provincial city on [Arrhenos](../../worlds/arrhenos/index.md), a wealthy banking world inhabited [entirely by men](../../worlds/arrhenos/women-and-gender.md). The technology around him is so far past his comprehension that his mind does the only thing it knows how to do with overwhelming, unparseable input: it builds a game out of it. He sees a heads-up display. Health bars. Skill trees. Loot. To survive he learns to play a world that is not a game — and, more slowly and more dangerously, to stop playing the people in it.

## Genre

A **LitRPG portal fantasy** that is, underneath, **science fiction with no fantasy in it at all**, carried by an **M/M romance** that does the emotional load-bearing. The LitRPG furniture is real to Milo and only to Milo; the SF is real to everyone; the romance is where the two finally have to be reconciled.

## The hook: a double exposure

The engine of the whole book is a single sustained irony — every scene is two scenes:

- **The world's reality.** No magic. Only [biotech, neural interfaces, and physics](../../setting/technology/enhancement.md) centuries beyond Milo. The locals read him instantly as an off-worlder: he has no [torc](../../worlds/arrhenos/society.md), which is roughly like having no face, no wallet, and no papers at once.
- **Milo's reading.** A sci-fi RPG interface laid over all of it — objective markers, rarity colours, dialogue trees, a class and a level. It is wrong about almost everything and useful anyway, because a wrong map you can act on beats a true world you cannot read.

The reader sees both layers at once and the comedy, tension, and pathos all live in the gap. The System is never confirmed as real and never quite dismissed as delusion; it earns its keep by working, and unsettles by working *too well*.

## The central conflict

Milo wants what he wanted on Earth — clear rules, a place where competence is legible and effort visibly pays off. The cruel joke is that [Arrhenos is exactly that world](../../worlds/arrhenos/society.md): an openly [meritocratic society](../../worlds/arrhenos/government.md) that runs on legible advancement. He got his wish and it nearly kills him, because the one credential that makes you a person here — the torc — is the one thing he cannot fake. Without it he is locked out of legitimate services and shunted into the grey economy, forced to live by scavenging, cover stories, and favours until he can either buy his way into legitimacy or be vouched for into it. See [outline.md](outline.md).

Worse, the one asset that should fix this — a [mint Earth phone](cast.md), a fortune in [antiques terms](../../worlds/arrhenos/locations/brovdingonai/shambles.md) — turns out to be a liability, not a windfall: too anomalous to sell cleanly, it ends up in a curious broker's hands and becomes a thread someone dangerous can pull. So money stays scarce, and scarcity drives Milo into the city's grey underside — into [Lex's](cast.md) world of un-logged couriering — where his one real talent finally pays, and where the [intrigue](outline.md) that defines Book 1 is waiting.

The deeper conflict is the System itself. It is his life-raft and his cage: it lets him function, and it keeps the world (and the men in it) at the safe distance of numbers. The arc of the book is the slow, contested business of putting it down — complicated by the fact that, [unlike a delusion](system.md), some of what it shows him is real.

## Themes

- **Legibility and its price.** A society that makes everything about a person readable — merit, history, money, identity, all routed through the torc. Milo, by contrast, is a man with no readable record at all, and spends the book deciding how much of himself to make legible, and to whom.
- **Coping as competence.** The System is a trauma response that happens to be adaptive. The book treats the gamified mind with sympathy, not mockery — and then asks what it costs to keep it.
- **Loneliness in a crowd.** Arrhenos is [two billion men](../../worlds/arrhenos/index.md) and, per its own canon, [isolation is one of its real themes](../../worlds/arrhenos/intimacy.md). Milo arrives more alone than anyone on the planet. Connection is the thing the System cannot quantify, and the thing he actually needs.
- **The default that isn't a struggle.** Here, [a man loving a man is as unremarkable as gravity](../../worlds/arrhenos/intimacy.md). The romance carries no closet, no coming-out, no external prejudice. Its obstacles are entirely human: Milo's secret, his distance, his habit of treating affection as a stat to grind.

## Tone

Wry and warm over a genuine ache. The System sections run dry, clipped, faintly absurd — the cold numbers. The human sections run close, specific, emotionally exact — the warmth. The book lives on the contrast and should never let the gamification cheapen a feeling; when the two registers collide, the human one wins. Comedy is welcome; cruelty toward Milo's coping is not. See [setting-brief.md](setting-brief.md) for the Arrhenos-specific tonal guardrails (this is a civilised banking republic, **not** a gritty prison planet).

## Audience and form

Adult readers of LitRPG and SF romance, comfortable with serialised long-form fiction that rewards patience and continuity. The form is **iterative**: written in instalments, each appending a [State Card](system-state-card.md) and updating the [bible](bible.md), built so threads planted early pay off late. See [craft.md](craft.md) and [direction.md](direction.md).
